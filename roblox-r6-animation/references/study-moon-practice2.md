## What makes it read as professional

In order of weight, with the evidence above:

1. **Every key is an extreme.** Wide stances, deep crouches, twisted torsos, limbs far from the body. No key is a neutral pose.
2. **Snap and hold.** Moves between keys take 0.03 to 0.15 s (the whip one capture frame, the release 0.07 s, the lunge 0.13 s, the draw 0.15 s). The holds between them take 0.17 to 0.9 s and drift.
3. **Anticipation in stages.** The throw loads, winds, winds further, then whips. The leap crouches, hops, hangs, then lunges.
4. **Weight by character.** The heavy golem skids 0.3 s, recovers 0.6 to 0.9 s and stays low. The light noob recoils in 0.12 s and spends its time in the air.
5. **Arcs of the body, not only the hand.** Entrances fall in, landings settle in an arc, the orb and the noob fly on curves.
6. **Smears hide the fastest frames.** A swoosh on the blade, streaks in the body colours on a dash, stretched legs on a launch, scrape lines on a skid, radial lines at a landing. Each lasts one to three capture frames.
7. **Impact frames only on the three biggest hits.** Five to seven unique frames (at the 30 fps capture) of inverted colour, flat colour and silhouettes, then a white flash.
8. **The camera is part of the animation.** It follows with a slight lag, drops low for the heavy character, cuts on the contact frame, holds an empty frame before an entrance, and lets bodies and objects pass the lens.
9. **Breathers.** After each climax the shot holds 1.3 to 1.9 s (smoke, a stare). The stare on the eyes is nearly still for 1.15 s.
10. **Comedy in the timing.** A bomb flies out of the smoke at the lens; the noob pops out of the cloud; the reveal comes after a hold.

## Lessons from "Make Your Roblox Animations Feel REAL" (Devgrams, 2026)

Read from the transcript; the frames were not downloaded.

- Anticipation: move the key back and add a wind-back before the arm rises, so the move reads.
- Drag: bend the limb and delay it as it rises, then let it catch up.
- Follow-through: the impact pose, an overshoot past it, then a return. "Nothing stays in the same place."
- Motion paths: turn on motion paths and fix the arcs before the timing.
- A sword M1: the torso leans in on the way in and leans out on the way out, overshoots and comes back; the arm follows the torso; the arms are not lined up with the torso.
- Practice by rotoscoping reference (a game, a film, a baseball throw) limb for limb. It teaches posing and timing. Do not publish copied animation as your own.
- Keep an animation simple until the basics read.

## Lessons from "12 Principles of Animation" (Alan Becker, 2017)

Read from the transcript. The points that principles.md did not already carry:

- Anticipation can have levels: a wind-out before the wind-up, then the other arm thrown back before the punch.
- Staging: the main action in the centre or on a third, room in the direction the character faces, one action at a time, a pause when the viewer needs to take something in.
- Pose to pose works in levels: keys first, then extremes (the farthest point in each direction), then breakdowns, then in-betweens.
- Straight ahead suits hair, cloth and effects; add them after the body.
- Standing up: offset the top half from the bottom half.
- Slow in and slow out only where the motion really eases: no slow in on a ball hitting the ground or a bullet leaving a gun.
- A body lands and settles in an arc; a head turn dips in an arc; a step moves the body down and up before the foot leaves.
- A smear is a filled arc between two poses in the object's colour, slightly transparent or broken at the end.
- Timing changes meaning: no in-between reads as a hit, one as a blow, three as a dodge, ten as a slow stretch.
- On twos gives snap to fast actions; on ones reads a flurry.
- A fast extreme that lasts one frame reads weaker than its still frame. Hold it longer or push it further. Push until it is too much, then pull back.
