## The grip is a joint with three axes

R6 has no visible wrist: the arm block ends and the handle leaves the fist at any angle, and the part of the handle that would be inside the forearm hides inside the arm block. So weld the weapon with a Motor6D from the arm (`SwordGrip`, C0 at the fist `(0, -0.9, 0)` with a rotation that maps the handle's axes to the arm's, C1 at the grip point on the handle) and key it as a Poser joint (the joint name is the handle part's name). Give it all three rotations. The hard limit is the blade folding back up into the forearm (blade direction against the arm's up axis); penalise that. The fist rule below adds the soft limit that makes the grip read.

A solver that limited the wrist to human ranges (45 degrees of deviation) missed the hand by 0.5 to 0.9 studs on most swing keys: the targets asked for a blade in line with the arm, which every R6 sword clip does.

## Key the weapon in world terms and solve the arm

Write each key as a hand point and a blade direction (and optionally the direction the leading edge faces) in root space, and solve the arm (lift, twist, side) and the grip (three angles) with Nelder-Mead: `WeaponRig.solveSword(tp, hand, blade, edge, seed)`. Rules that made the solutions usable:

- A rigid arm cannot reach a point closer than its length (1.49 from the shoulder pivot to the fist), so match the hand by its direction from the shoulder and put the fist at arm's length; slide the arm in the socket (p) by at most 0.32 for the rest. Keep one exact point only where a contact needs it (a grab on the hilt).
- Keep continuity by the physical rotation from the previous key's arm and grip, not by channel differences. Several arm solutions look alike at a key and whip the weapon around between them.
- A forehand contact reads with the fist front-right and the blade crossing forward-left and down; a backhand contact with the arm forward-left and the blade forward-right rising. A blade target nearly parallel to the arm is where a bad solution starts.
- The other hand on the handle is a reach solve to a point on the handle (`solveReach`).
- The left hand is the right solver mirrored: `WeaponRig.solveLeft` solves the X-mirrored problem with `solveSword` and mirrors the result back (arm and wrist channels `{lift, -twist, -side}`, `p.X` negated; the pose is Rz * Rx * Ry, and a mirror across X keeps Rx and flips Ry and Rz). Keep the left seed in the mirrored space.
- Solving every key at require time took 4.8 s. Memoize the solver by its inputs and bake the results into a module (`WeaponRig.dumpCache`, 54 entries): a fresh build then takes 0.03 to 0.07 s with no misses. Round fresh results the way the cache stores them, or the next lookup string differs.

## The arm's physical twist, not the twist channel

The R6 shoulder pivots on the arm's inner top edge, so twisting the arm about its own length swings the whole block around that edge: at 180 degrees the arm sits inside the torso. Limit the physical twist (`WeaponRig.physTwist`: a swing-twist split against the same arm swung straight from pointing forward), soft 35 and hard 65 degrees. Limiting the Euler twist channel does not work: the same pose on the other Euler branch reads a small twist channel and a 180 degree physical twist.

## Euler branches and the spline

Poser splines each Euler channel. `{l, t, s}` and `{180 - l, t + 180, s + 180}` are the same rotation, so two keys that look alike can sit on different branches and the spline spins the joint the long way round between them. Measured: a sword tip dove 2.5 studs under the floor between two nearly identical grip keys. After all keys are made:

1. Rewrite every key on the branch nearest the previous key (`WeaponRig.nearestEuler`), and run the pass again after any key is inserted (a single pass before an insertion left one key on the old branch).
2. Between two arm or grip keys more than 45 degrees apart, insert a slerp midpoint so the spline takes the short way.

Gimbal poses hide the same trap: an arm at `{90, 0, 0}` equals `{90, t, -t}` for any t. Write it on the branch of the keys around it (for example `{90, 88, -88}`) or the spline spins the arm the long way.

## A fist holds the handle across the forearm

- **The rule.** A real fist wraps across the handle, so the handle runs roughly square to the forearm (a blade to forearm angle of about 65 to 115 degrees). On a block R6 arm a handle more than about 25 degrees off square looks like it is run through the arm, because the block has no hand to hide the angle. A solver with only a fold-back limit puts poses such as "arm down and out, blade level along the arm line" 45 degrees off.
- **In the solver.** Penalise `abs(handleUp . armUp) > 0.42` (60 times the excess squared) and log the angle per key (`gripErr`, `leftGripErr`). In a two-handed grip search, hold the right forearm within 20 degrees of square (`abs((rh - SR).Unit . b) <= 0.34`) and prefer the left within 30.
- **In the poses.** Pick the arm direction and the blade so they are square before solving: a rest with the blade level out to the side needs the arm hanging nearly straight down (15 degrees out gave 6 degrees off square); a blade to the sky needs the arm out level to the side, not straight up; a cut that ends across the front needs the blade pointing across the body, not along the forearm. Measured after: rest 6 degrees, combo swings 3 to 15, the worst keys (wind-ups behind the body, a fast draw) 21 to 26.
- **The second fist.** With R6 shoulders 2 studs apart the left arm usually runs along the handle to reach the pommel end (55 to 77 degrees off square), and that reads as correct. The leading fist is the one that reads.
- **A sweep.** A horizontal sweep turns the blade 180 degrees against the forearm; where the blade points forward the grip lies along the arm (33 degrees off square measured). Keep that a single fast frame; keep every held key at 25 degrees or less.
- **Never counter-rotate in the fist.** Turning the handle inside the fist to hold a world angle (up to 65 degrees against an arm swing was tried) reads as a loose grip. Keep the handle within about 20 degrees of square and steer the blade with the arm.

## One grip for every held pose

A solver that picks the grip joint per key turns the blade in the fist between poses, and each pose reads as a new grip. Lock the grip joint to the grip already accepted (for example the idle's) and solve only the arm. With a locked grip, the angle between the shoulder-to-fist line and the blade never changes (108 degrees on one rig), so each blade target allows only a cone of fist directions: project the wanted fist direction onto that cone before the solve and the hand and blade errors are 0. Measure the cone with the arm slide at zero; a carry that slides the arm off the joint gives a false angle.

A long blade limits the turns: with 4.9 studs from the fist to the tip and the shoulder 4.4 above the floor, the blade can never hang straight down, so a turn from a back carry to a forward guard goes level around the side (arm up, twisting about the vertical) or over the top. When a pose moves a sword from a shoulder carry to a low ready pose, key a midpoint with the fist out to the side and the blade still pointing back, or the interpolation lifts it overhead.

## Measure the blade, look at an onion skin

`Poser.check` sees the body. For a weapon also sample per frame: the lowest blade tip height (under the floor means a flip or a bad key), the fastest blade turn per frame (a spike far over the strike frames means a spin), the grip angle, and the miss and blade error of every solved key. Take the blade direction as the hand-to-tip vector; a part's LookVector can point at the pommel. Good clips ended with tips 0.15 or more above the floor and the fastest turns 42 to 73 degrees per frame, only on the strike frames.

A strip of ghosts is too small to read a swing from. An onion skin (`_G.astaOnion(clip, bodies, t0, t1)` in the greatsword example, `_G.samOnion` in the three-sword kit) puts one or two bodies on one root and a faded, colour-ramped copy of the weapon every frame from t0 to t1. Capture it from above (the arc), from the front three quarter (the height of the arc) and from behind (the player's view).

## Weight: a heavy blade, two hands, a step every swing

- **What reads as a toy.** One-handed whips, the blade changing direction as fast as the arm, a wrist flick back to the shoulder, and the body standing still under the swing.
- **What reads as weight.** The body loads first and leads: the torso turns 1 to 2 frames before the arms and 2 frames before the grip (`lag` of 0.01 to 0.03 on both arms and the grip on the load and top beats, none on the contact beat). A slow load (warp 0.7 to 0.85), a fast strike (1.45), a contact hang (0.4), a follow-through where the blade drags the body down (crouch 0.36 to 0.44) and the tip scrapes the floor, and a heave: the legs push up, the torso leans back and both hands lift the blade in front before it goes onto the shoulder. The next swing starts from the drag, not from a reset.
- **Range of motion.** Force comes from a big wind-up and a big follow-through of the whole body and the weapon. A blade that turns at the wrist while the hands stay in front of the chest reads as weak. Lay the blade out behind for the load (torso space `(0.28, 0.3, 0.91)` with the torso twisted 56 right and leaning back 12) and carry the follow-through past the front (torso twisted 64 left). Check the hand travel, the blade arc and the torso range of every strike before tuning timing.
- **The floor limits the angle.** With the fists about 2.2 studs above the floor, a 6.7 stud blade can point only about 17 degrees down before the tip reaches the floor. A torso lean adds its angle to the blade. So a contact blade is nearly level (world pitch -8 to -20 degrees), and a drag or a slam bites at most 0.3.
- **A step every swing without sliding.** The clip carries `root(t)`, a monotone cubic of studs travelled forward, and the owning client pushes the root with a plane `LinearVelocity` at `root'(t) * warp * speed` along the facing the move started with (0 during a hitstop or a stun). Every sole stays planted in the world while the root travels: in root space its z grows by the travel. Only inside its step windows (or an air window) does a foot float, and it lands where the rest of the travel carries it back onto its stance spot by the time it lifts again. The lead foot steps in on the load and strike, the rear foot snaps up after the hit, and the stance is whole again at the chain time. Measured travel per swing: 1.15 to 1.30 for a cut, 2.48 for a leap, 2.95 for a spin. A rigid R6 leg reaches about 1.1 studs back from the hip at a 0.35 crouch, so the root travels at most 0.8 before the rear foot lifts. The foot check must judge a planted sole in the world (root space minus the travel), or every planted sole reads as sliding.
- **Toes follow the hips.** A floating foot turns its toe late in the step (`u * u`), and the planted feet pivot with the hips on the load and the follow-through, or the leg twists 57 to 78 degrees against the torso. Keep the twist of a planted leg against the torso under about 45 degrees. A pivot that ends after the hips (0.03 to 0.2 against a wind-up that peaks at 0.12) read 51 degrees; retimed to 0.02 to 0.12 it read 45.
- **Measured after the pass.** Swings rest 43 to 47% (48 to 54% in real time with the contact hangs), contrast 2.7 to 4.3, stops 2.0 to 2.5 a second; soles within 0.06, slide 0.06 or less in the world, hip gap 0.09 or less, twist 45 or less; tips 0.05 to 0.16 above the floor except a slam's 0.32 bite.

## Reach limits: two hands, overhead, a spear

- **Two hands on a sword.** A fist reaches 1.49 studs from its shoulder (plus a 0.3 slide), the shoulders are 2 studs apart, and the second fist sits 1.05 up the handle toward the pommel (`lGrip` in `WeaponRig.body`). So in torso space a two-handed blade never points to the far side of the body (the pommel fist would cross over), and a blade pointing straight forward puts the pommel fist inside the chest (`b.z` below -0.66). Key the blade in torso space, let a small Nelder-Mead place the grip (both fists within reach, both at least 0.62 in front of the torso centre, near a preferred spot in front of the chest), and let the torso twist turn the blade to the world direction. The second fist stayed within 0.16 of the handle on every key. At contact the blade points forward-right in torso space; a 30 to 55 degree torso twist to the left points it at the target.
- **Overhead.** The fist grip point is 1.487 from the shoulder joint (joint at torso (1, 0.5), grip at arm offset (0.5, -1.4)). Its highest point is torso y 1.99, straight over the shoulder; the head top is 2.0. Over the head centre it reaches only y 1.6 (forehead), and in front of the face less. Two fists on a vertical handle above the head need the arms slid out of the shoulders. Say this before building an overhead pose and offer the options: slide the arms up with a visible gap, the maximum reach at forehead height, or one hand. A pose that works: fists in front of the forehead (arm direction (-0.8, 0.45, -0.4) from the right shoulder in root space), the blade vertical, the second fist 0.8 below on the handle (`lGrip = 0.8`); grips 10 to 21 degrees off square, second-hand miss 0.24 or less.
- **Spear or lance.** An R6 hand reaches 1.58 studs from its shoulder pivot, and the pivots are 2 studs apart. Both hands reach one shaft only when the rear hand sits at the chest centre (rear arm about 78 degrees across the chest) and the shaft runs 25 to 45 degrees across the chest toward the front-hand side. Straight forward from the chest leaves a 0.46 stud gap. Turn the torso side-on (yaw about 35 degrees toward the rear hand) so the crossed shaft points at the target; key the arms and the shaft in the torso-yaw frame, so the yaw alone aims the weapon and a sweep is the whole body turning. Solve the front hand onto the shaft every frame (reach sphere against the shaft line, grip point 1.2 to 3.4 studs ahead of the rear hand); fall back to a free arm channel with a weight when the pose cannot reach. The arms cannot extend a thrust: slide the shaft through the hands (up to about 1 stud) and lunge the torso and the root instead. From the player's camera a thrust from the chest centre hides behind the body; judge it from the side and let the VFX carry it in the player view.

## A long tool held by the engine grip

A `Tool` gets a `RightGrip` weld from the engine on equip (C0 `CF(0, -1, 0) * Rx(-90)` on the right arm, C1 = `Tool.Grip`).

- **The grip is not a Poser joint.** Keep the tool and blend the weld: every client that plays the clip writes `RightGrip.C1 = Tool.Grip:Lerp(actionGrip, w(t))` each frame (w up over 0.02 to 0.13 s, down over 1.03 to 1.17 s) and puts `Tool.Grip` back at the end. The carry grip stays for walking.
- **A square fist cannot reach the floor with a long handle.** With the handle square to the arm, a tool head on the floor ahead needs the arm raised forward-up. A floor tool (a mop) runs about 20 degrees off the arm line with the fist 0.6 up the handle (`CF(0, 0.6, 0) * Rz(-20) * Rx(-70)`); the handle above the fist hides inside the arm block.
- **Two hands on a long handle.** A search over torso, arm and grip found the reachable family: the right fist at the chest centre (arm about `{15, 87, -90}`), the torso leaning 22 to 26 forward and turned right 15 to 43, the tool head 3 to 3.6 studs ahead and left of centre. The left hand is a post pass that aims the left arm at a handle point 1.2 below the right fist every frame (slide up to 0.3), blended in over 0.18 s and out over 0.2 s; a 0.11 s blend read as a snap (16 degrees a frame).
- **The body drives the stroke.** The solved arm keys change by only a few degrees; the torso twist carries the head across 1.6 studs. Solve each key for the head on its floor target with the torso twist and drop free and the hip gap in the cost.
- **Keep the head out of the floor between keys.** Straight from the carry pose to the first floor key the head dug 0.69 under the floor. Breakdowns solved with the grip weight of their time, seeded from the slerp midpoint of the neighbour keys, keep it above. Measure the lowest head corner every frame.
- **Hand back to a running Animator.** When the default Animate still plays under the clip, `Rig:stop` fades to identity and the Animator's pose then pops in. Copy `rig.last`, `stop(0)`, then for 0.15 s write `from:Lerp(motor.Transform, w)` in PreSimulation (the Animator's pose of that frame). Measured in Play: no joint spike at the end.
- **Replication.** Transforms are local, so the server fires a remote with the character and every other client plays the same clip on it.

## A combo flows from the end of the last swing

- **The fault.** Each swing designed as a good move on its own, from its own ideal load. In a chain, the first frames of the next swing undo the end of the last one (a blade high over one shoulder carried back across the head to a load over the other), and the detour reads as a weird pose even when every key is clean.
- **The rule.** Plan the chain as one path before any key. Key 0 of swing N+1 is swing N's end or hold pose (the same torso, head and blade in torso space), and its first keys carry the blade on in the direction it was travelling or round on its own arc: never back through the guard, never across to a new load. The end of a swing is the load of the next. If a wanted swing needs a load the last swing does not end in, change the last swing's follow-through, not the start of the next one. Start each clip's feet where the last one held them and step them back inside the new clip.
- **An example chain as one path.** Swing 1 chops from the blade laid out behind the right shoulder down across the front and ends low left. Swing 2 starts low left and rises up the diagonal to the blade vertical over the right shoulder. Swing 3 starts there: the body sinks while the blade goes on back over the head, jumps, and drives the blade straight down in front. Swing 4 starts from the slam's end. Check it in Play with a hold on the last swing's end and on the next swing's early keys (0.1, 0.25 and the contact) from the side: the blade must keep travelling one way.
- **A held combo for rhythm.** When players should chain at their own rhythm, end each swing in a moving hold of its end pose (1.5 s worked) before the recovery. Give the hold a middle breath key (the blade lifts a little, the torso rises) and an end key a touch further along the move, with staggered lags (torso -0.08, head +0.1, grip +0.12) so the joints never breathe together; life 0.9. The next swing starts from the hold pose, so a click anywhere in the hold chains cleanly. The combo window equals the hold (the input and the server use the same number), and a finisher's cooldown counts from its chain time, not from the end of its hold. Measure the swing part (up to the chain time) against the one-shot ranges; over the whole clip the hold reads 28 to 55% still but never frozen. Rhythm test: clicks 1.4, 0.7 and 1.9 s apart all chained.
- **Measured after.** Grips 15 degrees off square or less, leg twist 46 or less; in a 60 fps step of every move the only single-frame peaks are the strike frames (blade 60 to 86 degrees a frame with the torso and both arms at their peak on the same frame, then the contact hang).

## Slashes that read

- **A slash is radial.** A still of a horizontal slash reads as a thrust when the blade points the way it travels. The blade must stick out from the body so it moves broadside; with a square grip on a block R6 arm that means the arm angled down and out (pitch about -60) with the blade level, or the arm up. The same contact solved with the arm straight out gave a tangential blade that read as a stab.
- **A horizontal slash keeps the wrist.** Solve the contact key freely (hand, blade and edge), then hold that grip for every beat of the swing and solve only the arm, with each beat's blade target made by turning the contact blade by the same yaw as the hand. The blade then sweeps with the arm like a lever. Freely solved grips per beat differed by up to 150 degrees and the spline dragged the tip 2.3 studs under the floor. The edge target is the tangent of the sweep (at the contact of a right-to-left cut the edge faces left-back and the flat faces up).
- **The edge leads.** In a cut or a slam the edge faces the direction of travel. A blade held flat to the front looks wide from a front camera; turn the edge from the side to forward during the wind-up (0.28 s), which on a big blade reads as a beat of its own. "Sharp side up" means the blade stands on its edge with the flat to the front (rest edge `(0, 1, 0)` in torso space). Treat the edge as a soft preference in the solver (weight 10 against 40 for the blade) and log the edge error per key: 1 to 7 degrees on swings, under 10 at rest.
- **Hidden contact frames.** From a camera straight behind, a blade that crosses 4 to 5 studs in front at shoulder height hides inside the torso silhouette at the contact frame; the slash VFX must carry that frame.
- **A shield strapped to the forearm** sits on the outer face: C0 about (-0.72, -0.55, 0) on the left arm with the shield face along arm -X. Aim the forearm across the chest and add a twist term so the outer face points where the shield should face.

## A weapon in locomotion

- **Welded in idle and walk.** In a locomotion loop the weapon joint holds one constant pose: the mean grip of the idle, shared by the idle and the walk so their blend never turns the blade in the fist. A traced clip that keys the weapon on its own drifts a few degrees against the arm and reads as a floating weapon. A carry may slide the arm out of its shoulder joint, but the arm box must touch the torso box on every frame: measure the gap per frame and slide the arm onto the torso side (`scripts/blender/contact_fix_bg.py` does this on a channel JSON).
- **A weapon arm in a run pumps.** A frozen weapon arm next to a pumping free arm reads wrong (a 14 degree pump read as frozen). Pump the weapon arm opposite the free arm at about 60% of its range (2 to 60 degrees against the torso measured, -18 to +36 in the world with the lean), one frame behind the free arm, crossing in about 10 degrees and sliding 0.16 up the shoulder on the forward swing for an elbow cue. A long blade needs that smaller swing, which also reads as the weapon's weight.
- **Tip clearance from the wrist, not a frozen arm.** Let the wrist give up to 15 degrees against the swing (grip lift +15 with the arm back, -14 with the arm forward; grip 14.9 degrees off square) so the blade rocks with the fist; bias the swing forward. Check the tip height over the cycle (at least 1.38 to 1.56 studs above the floor measured) and remember the torso lean adds to the blade's world angle.
- **Which way the blade points in a run is a design choice.** A trailing blade and a blade held forward (the arm forward at the waist cancelling a 22 degree lean, blade pitch -7.4 to +4.3, heading within 6 degrees of straight ahead) both work. Ask before choosing.
- **A held weapon in a pumping arm is seen best** from a rear three-quarter on the weapon side (58 degrees, 10 studs back, 2.4 up); straight behind, a forward blade and back pieces (a shield, a cape) hide.

## Cinematic moves: speed curves, jumps, spins and root motion

- **Speed curve, not rekeying.** A clip's `warp` (pipeline.md) plays its own clock faster or slower. What read well: the load at 0.75 to 0.85, the strike at 1.3 to 1.5, a hang of 0.3 to 0.45 for 0.03 to 0.05 clip seconds right after the contact, then 1.0 for the recovery. A hero hold in a draw at 0.35 for 0.12 clip seconds (0.34 s real). A strain on a grip at 0.55 with a tremble post layer (noise at 22 per second, 3 degrees on the arm, 1.4 on the torso) so the slow part is not a freeze. Keep hit, lock and chain times on the server from `Poser.realTime`, never from hand-typed seconds.
- **A jump without moving the root.** Lift the torso and lift both feet with an air curve that is at least the rise (0.3 to 0.4 more tucks the legs up into the hips). A lift curve behind the torso opens a hip gap on the takeoff frame (0.12 at 0.15 s); start the lift as the legs straighten. Hand targets in root space must rise with the body, or the arm hangs below the shoulder and the blade tip goes under the floor (0.52 under at an apex). A split in the air (the front foot 0.45 forward, the back foot 0.75 back) reads as a leap; straight legs read as a float.
- **Spins past 360.** Key the torso twist straight through (-68, -12, 75, 165, 255, 325, 388) and keep the Euler branch fix off the torso and head, only on the arms and the grip. Key the blade in torso space so it rides the spin, with the edge along the spin (the spin axis crossed with the blade). The feet turn round the root with the torso while airborne; land with the chest 25 to 35 degrees past the hips, then pivot one foot at a time back to the stance. A blade held level in torso space points down once the torso leans forward (at a 19 degree lean it went 1.67 under the floor); tilt it up by the lean.
- **Spin, dash and travel as clip fields.** For big root motion, give the clip functions of clip time and let the owner client place the root in PreRender: `spin(t)` returns the root yaw (a monotone cubic, so the yaw never runs backward; `K.monotone` in the three-sword kit), `dash = {t0, t1}` moves the root over a raycast-clamped distance with an ease-out, and `root(t)` returns height, forward distance and yaw. A leap is a parabola `h * 4u(1 - u)` so it lands with speed, not an eased stop; a spin that ends on a multiple of 360 faces the target at the release. Feet keys stay in root space, so the air tuck and the landing lunge ride with the root. A 1440 degree spin then needs no per-turn keys; `Poser.check` reads rest high (67%) because the root, not the joints, carries the motion. Key a dash's crossing pose at the middle of the window and the arrival past the target.
- **Whole-body turn and flip channels.** A `turn = {{t, deg}}` (yaw) and `flip = {{t, deg}}` (pitch) pair that rotates only the root joint (Torso pose = turn * flip * body) lets the arm, weapon and leg poses ride along with no extra keys; author the body as if the channel were zero. A clip that ends in a hover ends the channel on a multiple of 360. After a spin, key the torso yaw from the facing you want minus the turn modulo 360, or the follow-through keeps rotating. Rotate a flip around the centre of mass (lesson-videos.md, physics).
- **Busy time.** Let the server's busy window end before the clip ends (the real length minus 0.3 s worked) so the next move can start while the recovery settles.
- **Numbers.** Swings 28 to 47% rest in real time with contrast 3.3 to 5.8; a long cinematic draw or sheathe sits at 62 to 67% rest in real time because of its deliberate slow beats (still 3% or less, frozen 0).

## More than two weapons, and props on the head

- **A free-model blade as a grip joint.** Weld the model to a small invisible handle part named for the joint and scale it about the handle (0.45 made a 5.8 stud katana). For a katana, handle frame +Y from the guard to the pommel and +Z along the edge; the edge sits on the convex side of the curve. Put the fist so the guard stays outside it (origin 0.18 ahead of the guard toward the pommel, `GRIP_Y` 0.35; C0 `(0, -0.9, 0)`, C1 `CF(0, 0.35, 0) * GRIP_R:Inverse()`).
- **A prop on the head** (a blade held in the mouth) is a Motor6D on the Head: C0 `CFrame.fromMatrix((0, -0.24, -0.5), (0, 1, 0), (1, 0, 0))` (pommel to the right, blade out to the left, edge forward), C1 `CF(0, 0.45, 0)` so the guard clears the cheek. It rides the neck; head yaw swings it (+28 turns the blade back for a load, -22 forward for a strike).
- **A stance loop over the Animator's legs.** A stance that keys only the head, the arms and the grips lets the default walk drive the legs. End the move on the loop's first pose with the torso and legs at rest (feet at x +-0.5, z 0) so the hands come back with no snap.
- **Readability from behind.** Blades raised on wide fists with the edge inward (the tips curve out) read as wings from the player's camera; blades pointing back point at the camera and vanish.
- **Low blades after a dash.** A low flick after a dash put a tip through the floor; raise the blade direction (y -0.12 with z -0.99 held it clear) and check the tip on the onion skin.
- **A cast mark from the root joint.** A watcher that fires when the root joint's Transform leaves identity fires again mid-move when a clip passes through identity (in a spin); take the first mark after the key press as the cast start.

## Props that grow, giant props and runtime cost

- **A prop that comes out of another prop.** A hilt rising out of a thin book hangs its guard and tang out of the bottom of the book on every frame before it is fully out. Grow it instead: scale the prop uniformly from 0 to 1 with its lower end pinned to the page, and grow the blade (a MeshPart scaled along its length only) so the tip stays on the page while the hand pulls. The fist then grips the grown hilt, so present the source prop lower (hip height) and further out. A thrown weapon that returns: fly an anchored copy from the release frame (captured from the real handle), let it climb and turn over, drop point first, and shrink the blade with its tip pinned.
- **A giant prop is not the welded weapon.** Resizing a part welded into a character rebuilds the assembly every frame (25 to 30 ms frames for a quarter second). Draw a growing or giant part as an anchored copy placed at the welded part's frame each frame, hide the welded one, and switch back when it stops changing. Low-pass its direction (0.3 s while it grows, 0.05 s in a slam) and clamp how far below level it may point, so a 2 degree wrist error does not swing a 384 stud tip 13 studs.
- **Grow, hold, slam as three clips.** A cutscene grow (clip time = real time, events on the reference's shot changes) whose `onDone` plays a looping hold clip (a breath key and a strain tremble on the arms) that stays until the slam replaces it. The hold aims by turning the root toward the camera at a limited rate while the Humanoid's AutoRotate is off.

## The twitch when a move hands back to the idle

Two causes, found in order:

1. **Snaps inside the clip.** A sheathe flick turned the torso 36 degrees in one frame (two keys 0.04 apart after a slow warp hold, with opposing torso lags), and a draw's flourish started on the same frame as a leg step. Fix: space the keys, drop the opposing lags, ease the warp out of the hold.
2. **The hand-over itself, which an Edit-mode step never shows.** The Humanoid's Animator zeroes every `Motor6D.Transform` between PreAnimation and PreSimulation (a probe read the right shoulder at 13.6 degrees in PreAnimation and PreRender and 0.0 in PreSimulation). A blend that captured the old pose from `motor.Transform` inside a step (an `onDone` hand-over) blended from the default pose: every joint stopped for one frame and then jumped (the right arm 27.9 degrees, the torso 9.8). `Poser` now keeps the pose each rig last wrote (`rig.last`) and blends from that.

**How to find it.** In Play, record each body part's CFrame relative to the root on every RenderStepped through the end of the move and one second of idle, turn it into degrees per 1/60 s and flag frames over 3 degrees and over 2.2 times both neighbours. Ignore a flag that follows a long frame (a hitch, not the animation).
