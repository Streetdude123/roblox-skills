## The grip is a joint with three axes

R6 has no visible wrist: the arm block ends and the handle leaves the fist at any angle, and the part of the handle that would be inside the forearm hides inside the arm block. So weld the weapon with a Motor6D from the arm (`SwordGrip`, C0 at the fist `(0, -0.9, 0)` with a rotation that maps the handle's axes to the arm's, C1 at the grip point on the handle) and key it as a Poser joint (the joint name is the handle part's name). Give it all three rotations. The hard limit is the blade folding back up into the forearm (blade direction against the arm's up axis); penalise that. The fist rule below adds the soft limit that makes the grip read.

A solver that limited the wrist to human ranges (45 degrees of deviation) missed the hand by 0.5 to 0.9 studs on most swing keys: the targets asked for a blade in line with the arm, which every R6 sword clip does.

## Key the weapon in world terms and solve the arm

Write each key as a hand point and a blade direction (and optionally the direction the leading edge faces) in root space, and solve the arm (lift, twist, side) and the grip (three angles) with Nelder-Mead: `WeaponRig.solveSword(tp, hand, blade, edge, seed)`. Rules that made the solutions usable:

- A rigid arm cannot reach a point closer than its length (1.49 from the shoulder pivot to the fist), so match the hand by its direction from the shoulder and put the fist at arm's length; slide the arm in the socket (p) by at most 0.32 for the rest. Keep one exact point only where a contact needs it (a grab on the hilt).
- Keep continuity by the physical rotation from the previous key's arm and grip, not by channel differences. Several arm solutions look alike at a key and whip the weapon around between them.
- On a body that leans or twists, write blade, aim and head-look directions in root space and convert them through the live torso rotation every frame. A root-space key holds its target under any twist. A torso-space key turns with the torso: a down-and-back blade came out level in the world at a 24 to 32 degree lean, and a level blade went 1.67 under the floor at a 19 degree lean. Key in torso space only where the blade must ride the torso (a spin, a two-handed aim by torso twist), and tilt it by the lean.
- A forehand contact reads with the fist front-right and the blade crossing forward-left and down; a backhand contact with the arm forward-left and the blade forward-right rising. A blade target nearly parallel to the arm is where a bad solution starts.
- The other hand on the handle is a reach solve to a point on the handle (`solveReach`).
- The left hand is the right solver mirrored: `WeaponRig.solveLeft` solves the X-mirrored problem with `solveSword` and mirrors the result back (arm and wrist channels `{lift, -twist, -side}`, `p.X` negated; the pose is Rz * Rx * Ry, and a mirror across X keeps Rx and flips Ry and Rz). Keep the left seed in the mirrored space.
- Solving every key at require time took 4.8 s. Memoize the solver by its inputs and bake the results into a module (`WeaponRig.dumpCache`): a fresh build then takes 0.03 to 0.07 s with 54 entries and 0.54 s with 361. Rebuild the cache after any clip edit and confirm 0 misses: a stale cache falls back to the slow solver (a rebuilt 361 entry cache gave 373 hits and 0 misses in Play). Round fresh results the way the cache stores them, or the next lookup string differs and misses.

## The arm's physical twist, not the twist channel

The R6 shoulder pivots on the arm's inner top edge, so twisting the arm about its own length swings the whole block around that edge: at 180 degrees the arm sits inside the torso. Limit the physical twist (`WeaponRig.physTwist`: a swing-twist split against the same arm swung straight from pointing forward), soft 35 and hard 65 degrees. Limiting the Euler twist channel does not work: the same pose on the other Euler branch reads a small twist channel and a 180 degree physical twist.

## Euler branches and the spline

Poser splines each Euler channel. `{l, t, s}` and `{180 - l, t + 180, s + 180}` are the same rotation, so two keys that look alike can sit on different branches and the spline spins the joint the long way round between them. Measured: a sword tip dove 2.5 studs under the floor between two nearly identical grip keys. After all keys are made:

1. Rewrite every key on the branch nearest the previous key (`WeaponRig.nearestEuler`), and run the pass again after any key is inserted (a single pass before an insertion left one key on the old branch).
2. Between two arm or grip keys more than 45 degrees apart, insert a slerp midpoint so the spline takes the short way.

Gimbal poses hide the same trap: an arm at `{90, 0, 0}` equals `{90, t, -t}` for any t. Write it on the branch of the keys around it (for example `{90, 88, -88}`) or the spline spins the arm the long way.

No branch fix saves an arm that points straight forward with the blade to the side: that pose sits at the gimbal of the arm's own Euler decomposition, and the channels flipped a blade 96 to 157 degrees in one frame. Drive an arm that crosses the forward line with non-Euler channels: a Hermite spline on the arc angle inside each arc and a vector spline of the arm and blade directions between keys, then build the frame from the vectors. Never let a keyed blade direction reverse between two keys: the interpolated vector passes through zero and the arm spins 100 to 170 degrees in a frame. Give a pose the blade of the arc that follows it, and route an arm over the head, not through the front, when the blade points along the arm's path.

## A fist holds the handle across the forearm

- **The rule.** A real fist wraps across the handle, so the handle runs roughly square to the forearm (a blade to forearm angle of about 65 to 115 degrees). On a block R6 arm a handle more than about 25 degrees off square looks like it is run through the arm, because the block has no hand to hide the angle. A solver with only a fold-back limit puts poses such as "arm down and out, blade level along the arm line" 45 degrees off.
- **In the solver.** Penalise `abs(handleUp . armUp) > 0.42` (60 times the excess squared) and log the angle per key (`gripErr`, `leftGripErr`). In a two-handed grip search, hold the right forearm within 20 degrees of square (`abs((rh - SR).Unit . b) <= 0.34`) and prefer the left within 30.
- **In the poses.** Pick the arm direction and the blade so they are square before solving: a rest with the blade level out to the side needs the arm hanging nearly straight down (15 degrees out gave 6 degrees off square); a blade to the sky needs the arm out level to the side, not straight up; a cut that ends across the front needs the blade pointing across the body, not along the forearm. Measured after: rest 6 degrees, combo swings 3 to 15, the worst keys (wind-ups behind the body, a fast draw) 21 to 26.
- **The second fist.** With R6 shoulders 2 studs apart the left arm usually runs along the handle to reach the pommel end (55 to 77 degrees off square), and that reads as correct. The leading fist is the one that reads.
- **Fast frames only.** A grip over 25 degrees off square passes only on fast frames, which go by before the angle reads: an airborne apex read fine at 27 to 29. Allow up to about 29 on fast frames and keep every held key at 25 degrees or less. A horizontal sweep is the one exception: it turns the blade 180 degrees against the forearm, and where the blade points forward the grip lies along the arm (33 degrees off square measured). Keep that a single fast frame.
- **Never counter-rotate in the fist.** Turning the handle inside the fist to hold a world angle (up to 65 degrees against an arm swing was tried) reads as a loose grip. Keep the handle within about 20 degrees of square and steer the blade with the arm.
- **A pointer is not a fist grip.** A wand or a pointer reads by extending the arm's line; the square-grip rule would aim it sideways. Give it its own Motor6D on the arm at the fist, along the arm's axis, and tilt it toward the arm's front with a wrist pitch.

## One grip for every held pose

A solver that picks the grip joint per key turns the blade in the fist between poses, and each pose reads as a new grip. Lock the grip joint to the grip already accepted (for example the idle's) and solve only the arm. With a locked grip, the angle between the shoulder-to-fist line and the blade never changes (108 degrees on one rig), so each blade target allows only a cone of fist directions: project the wanted fist direction onto that cone before the solve and the hand and blade errors are 0. Measure the cone with the arm slide at zero; a carry that slides the arm off the joint gives a false angle.

A long blade limits the turns: with 4.9 studs from the fist to the tip and the shoulder 4.4 above the floor, the blade can never hang straight down, so a turn from a back carry to a forward guard goes level around the side (arm up, twisting about the vertical) or over the top. When a pose moves a sword from a shoulder carry to a low ready pose, key a midpoint with the fist out to the side and the blade still pointing back, or the interpolation lifts it overhead. Print the tip height every frame of that move: it must only fall. Return the weapon to its carry along its outward path reversed unless the requester asks for another path; a requester picked the reversed path in every rebuild.

A calm guard from a shoulder carry: the fist comes from the shoulder toward the chest while the blade tips forward over the shoulder, at most about 45 degrees up, and ends level at chest height pointing forward. Slide the arm up 0.3 as an elbow hint. With a shield, raise it in front of the chest and the face. Print the blade elevation per frame: an arc that peaked at 54 degrees read unnatural, 40 to 42 passed, and only the print showed the peak. A commercial sword-and-shield reference showed this path.

## A blade resting on the body

- **A shoulder rest flips the arm.** With a square grip, a straight hanging arm runs the blade along the arm, so a straight arm cannot rest a blade on its own shoulder. Flip the arm: the fist at the shoulder, the arm down flush on the torso side, the blade back over the shoulder. Measured: the blade 0.07 above the shoulder, the grip 0.9 degrees off square, clear of the head. `scripts/examples/armored-warrior/offline/carry_search.py` searches this carry.
- **A wide blade rests flat forward.** On the shoulder, a 1.54 stud wide blade with the flat up cut the chest (62 samples, 0.44 deep); with the flat forward it cleared the arm top by 0.08.
- **A blade over its own shoulder** runs near the arm's line, because the fist is always 1.49 from that shoulder joint. That pose solved only to 25 to 26 degrees off square, so check its grip against the square limit. Lift a blade off a shoulder rest through an early breakdown straight up (at 0.07 s): Euler interpolation alone swept it behind the head.
- **A palm braces behind the flat.** Put a bracing hand 0.42 behind a wide blade's flat, not on its centre line: aimed at the centre line, the arm went through a 1.54 stud blade on 24 samples (2 after the change).
- **Rock a resting prop about its contact point.** A rotation about the prop's origin lifts or sinks the contact. Rotate by Q about the contact C and set the prop's offset to `t' = C + Q(p + t - C) - p` (p the prop's rest origin, t its offset, all in one frame), a couple of frames behind the part that drives it. A 4 degree rock kept a blade 0.001 to 0.007 above the shoulder on every sampled frame.

## Measure the blade, look at an onion skin

`Poser.check` sees the body. For a weapon also sample per frame: the lowest blade tip height (under the floor means a flip or a bad key), the fastest blade turn per frame (a spike far over the strike frames means a spin), the grip angle, and the miss and blade error of every solved key. Print them per frame, not per key: per-key summaries missed a 2-frame flip that sent a tip 8.5 up and a 61 degree one-frame snap. Take the blade direction as the hand-to-tip vector, never a local axis or a LookVector (it can point at the pommel). For a free-model prop, find the tip from the model's bounds and store its direction: a free-model union can be upside down in its own axes (a wand's tip was at local -Y). Good clips ended with tips 0.15 or more above the floor and the fastest turns 42 to 73 degrees per frame, only on the strike frames.

**A blade turn is not a joint limit.** The blade turn per frame is the weapon's world direction, which adds up the torso, arm and grip turns of the same frame, so 42 to 86 degrees on strike frames is normal. A single joint has its own limit when a retime squeezes a strike into fewer frames: retime the key gaps so each joint stays under about 25 degrees a frame (1500 deg/s) with the hit time pinned, or the strike falls into one frame. Squeezed punches reached 4683 deg/s (78 degrees a frame) on an arm before that pass; a boss rig's slam slowed to 0.17 to 0.18 s went from a 34 to a 26 to 28 degree peak per frame.

**The weapon against the body.** A tip check does not see a blade through the head: an overhead swing that passed its tip checks cut the head on 9 to 13 samples. On every frame, sample the blade as a plate on a 0.15 stud grid against the torso, head and arm boxes; for a prop that rests on the body, test the meshes (a BVH, or box penetration and gap). Search a clear pose with the real solver (contact point, direction, flat facing) until the cut is 0 and a rest gap is a few thousandths: a carry that looked right sat 0.20 inside the torso, and a search found a raise of 0.40 with -4 degrees of tilt (cut 0.000, gap 0.007). Keep each Studio call under about 250 solves; longer calls time out.

A strip of ghosts is too small to read a swing from. An onion skin (`_G.astaOnion(clip, bodies, t0, t1)` in the greatsword example, `_G.samOnion` in the three-sword kit) puts one or two bodies on one root and a faded, colour-ramped copy of the weapon every frame from t0 to t1. Capture it from above (the arc), from the front three quarter (the height of the arc) and from behind (the player's view).

## Weight: a heavy blade, two hands, a step every swing

- **What reads as a toy.** One-handed whips, the blade changing direction as fast as the arm, a wrist flick back to the shoulder, and the body standing still under the swing.
- **What reads as weight.** Mass lags its driver, so the body loads first and leads: the torso turns 1 to 2 frames before the arms and 2 frames before the grip (`lag` of 0.01 to 0.03 on both arms and the grip on the load and top beats, none on the contact beat). A slow load (warp 0.7 to 0.85), a fast strike (1.45), a contact hang (0.4), a follow-through where the blade drags the body down (crouch 0.36 to 0.44) and the tip scrapes the floor, and a heave: the legs push up, the torso leans back and both hands lift the blade in front before it goes onto the shoulder. The next swing starts from the drag, not from a reset. Use these with two hands, a planted step every swing, the held combo, full range and speed curves: a requester accepted that whole set after passes that read as a toy.
- **Range of motion.** Force comes from a big wind-up and a big follow-through of the whole body and the weapon. A blade that turns at the wrist while the hands stay in front of the chest reads as weak. Lay the blade out behind for the load (torso space `(0.28, 0.3, 0.91)` with the torso twisted 56 right and leaning back 12) and carry the follow-through past the front (torso twisted 64 left). Check the hand travel, the blade arc and the torso range of every strike before tuning timing.
- **The floor limits the angle.** Compute a long blade's lowest angle from the grip height and the length to the tip before keying. With the grip 1.4 to 2.2 studs above the floor, a 6.7 stud blade can point only about 9 to 17 degrees down before the tip reaches the floor; at 33 to 37 degrees the tip went 1.86 under. A torso lean adds its angle to the blade. So a contact blade is nearly level (world pitch -8 to -20 degrees), and a drag or a slam bites at most 0.3. A long blade points straight down only while airborne; a diving slam may bury the tip at a shallow angle (1.7 deep at 30 degrees). To trail a blade low with a square grip, hang the arm straight down: an arm angled back put the grip 26 to 31 degrees off square.
- **A step every swing without sliding.** The clip carries `root(t)`, a monotone cubic of studs travelled forward, and the owning client pushes the root with a plane `LinearVelocity` at `root'(t) * warp * speed` along the facing the move started with (0 during a hitstop or a stun). Every sole stays planted in the world while the root travels: in root space its z grows by the travel. Only inside its step windows (or an air window) does a foot float, and it lands where the rest of the travel carries it back onto its stance spot by the time it lifts again. The lead foot steps in on the load and strike, the rear foot snaps up after the hit, and the stance is whole again at the chain time. Measured travel per swing: 1.15 to 1.30 for a cut, 2.48 for a leap, 2.95 for a spin. A rigid R6 leg reaches about 1.1 studs back from the hip at a 0.35 crouch, so the root travels at most 0.8 before the rear foot lifts. The foot check must judge a planted sole in the world (root space minus the travel), or every planted sole reads as sliding. A sole placed before a travel phase is carried back by that travel unless it steps again (a 0.8 stud step ended 0.32 behind), so brace with the foot whose next step comes before the travel. In a travelling flurry give each strike one planted step: compute each planted foot's root-space z from the root curve and lift the swing foot on a sine. Five strikes 1.5 studs apart slid 0.04 or less.
- **Toes follow the hips.** A floating foot turns its toe late in the step (`u * u`), and the planted feet pivot with the hips on the load and the follow-through, or the leg twists 57 to 78 degrees against the torso. Keep the twist of a planted leg against the torso under about 45 degrees. A pivot that ends after the hips (0.03 to 0.2 against a wind-up that peaks at 0.12) read 51 degrees; retimed to 0.02 to 0.12 it read 45.
- **An overhead chop.** Force runs from the hips to the tip. Lift slowly and strike fast (0.40 s up, 0.08 s down): the blade goes back over the head, the torso arches back 13 to 14 degrees, the weight shifts back 0.1 on a small crouch (0.24), and a moving hold sits at the top. On the strike the torso pitches forward first and the arms and the blade follow 1 to 2 frames later; the front foot steps in and the back foot pivots on its ball as the hips drive (a 24 degree pivot cut the leg twist from 58 to 38 degrees). After the contact the blade's weight pulls the body lower.
- **A two-handed heavy load is a batter's stance.** Side-on (torso yaw about -60), the head turned 52 to 61 degrees to keep the eyes on the target, the body sunk (crouch 0.55) with the weight on the back leg, the back shoulder dropped, the feet wide along the target line and apart sideways so the stance shows from the player's camera. Hold the weapon flatter than a light bat (20 degrees up against 45): mass keeps a heavy weapon low.
- **A dash with a heavy weapon.** Load, push off, take two fast low steps with the blade trailing near the floor, let the blade overtake the body at the plant, then skid on both feet with a counter-lean. The blade lags the acceleration and overtakes on the braking like a mass; a plain slide did not read as grounded.
- **Measured after the pass.** Swings rest 43 to 47% (48 to 54% in real time with the contact hangs), contrast 2.7 to 4.3, stops 2.0 to 2.5 a second; soles within 0.06, slide 0.06 or less in the world, hip gap 0.09 or less, twist 45 or less; tips 0.05 to 0.16 above the floor except a slam's 0.32 bite.

## Reach limits: two hands, overhead, a spear

- **Two hands on a sword.** A fist reaches 1.49 studs from its shoulder (plus a 0.3 slide), the shoulders are 2 studs apart, and the second fist sits 1.05 up the handle toward the pommel (`lGrip` in `WeaponRig.body`). So in torso space a two-handed blade never points to the far side of the body (the pommel fist would cross over), and a blade pointing straight forward puts the pommel fist inside the chest (`b.z` below -0.66). Key the blade in torso space, let a small Nelder-Mead place the grip (both fists within reach, both at least 0.62 in front of the torso centre, near a preferred spot in front of the chest), and let the torso twist turn the blade to the world direction. The second fist stayed within 0.16 of the handle on every key. At contact the blade points forward-right in torso space; a 30 to 55 degree torso twist to the left points it at the target. A two-handed blade cannot point backward either: the square grip forces both arms up to the back ear (grip at torso y 1.6), and a hand-height weight in the solver does not move them. Load or take off one-handed with the blade low behind (grip 20 degrees off square), and bring the second hand on in the air or at the top of the swing (a leap joined the hands at the top of a 280 degree swing).
- **Overhead.** The fist grip point is 1.487 from the shoulder joint (joint at torso (1, 0.5), grip at arm offset (0.5, -1.4)). Its highest point is torso y 1.99, straight over the shoulder; the head top is 2.0. Over the head centre it reaches only y 1.6 (forehead), and in front of the face less. Two fists on a vertical handle above the head need the arms slid out of the shoulders. Say this before building an overhead pose and offer the options: slide the arms up with a visible gap, the maximum reach at forehead height, or one hand. A pose that works: fists in front of the forehead (arm direction (-0.8, 0.45, -0.4) from the right shoulder in root space), the blade vertical, the second fist 0.8 below on the handle (`lGrip = 0.8`); grips 10 to 21 degrees off square, second-hand miss 0.24 or less. A requester who asked for both fists above the head kept this pose once told the limit.
- **Spear or lance.** An R6 hand reaches 1.58 studs from its shoulder pivot, and the pivots are 2 studs apart. Both hands reach one shaft only when the rear hand sits at the chest centre (rear arm about 78 degrees across the chest) and the shaft runs 25 to 45 degrees across the chest toward the front-hand side. Straight forward from the chest leaves a 0.46 stud gap. Turn the torso side-on (yaw about 35 degrees toward the rear hand) so the crossed shaft points at the target; key the arms and the shaft in the torso-yaw frame, so the yaw alone aims the weapon and a sweep is the whole body turning. Solve the front hand onto the shaft every frame (reach sphere against the shaft line, grip point 1.2 to 3.4 studs ahead of the rear hand; measured gap 0.01 to 0.16 studs on every sampled frame); fall back to a free arm channel with a weight when the pose cannot reach. The arms cannot extend a thrust: slide the shaft through the hands (up to about 1 stud) and lunge the torso and the root instead. From the player's camera a thrust from the chest centre hides behind the body; judge it from the side and let the VFX carry it in the player view.
- **References for a two-handed prop.** Once both hands hold the prop, a one-handed swing or trick from a reference loses its motion and reads dead. Drop reference candidates whose action lives in the arms the grip locks, and say which survived and why.

## A long tool held by the engine grip

A `Tool` gets a `RightGrip` weld from the engine on equip (C0 `CF(0, -1, 0) * Rx(-90)` on the right arm, C1 = `Tool.Grip`).

- **The grip is not a Poser joint.** Keep the tool and blend the weld: every client that plays the clip writes `RightGrip.C1 = Tool.Grip:Lerp(actionGrip, w(t))` each frame (w up over 0.02 to 0.13 s, down over 1.03 to 1.17 s) and puts `Tool.Grip` back at the end. The carry grip stays for walking.
- **A square fist cannot reach the floor with a long handle.** With the handle square to the arm, a tool head on the floor ahead needs the arm raised forward-up. A floor tool (a mop) runs about 20 degrees off the arm line with the fist 0.6 up the handle (`CF(0, 0.6, 0) * Rz(-20) * Rx(-70)`); the handle above the fist hides inside the arm block.
- **Two hands on a long handle.** A search over torso, arm and grip found the reachable family: the right fist at the chest centre (arm about `{15, 87, -90}`), the torso leaning 22 to 26 forward and turned right 15 to 43, the tool head 3 to 3.6 studs ahead and left of centre. The left hand is a post pass that aims the left arm at a handle point 1.2 below the right fist every frame (slide up to 0.3), blended in over 0.18 s and out over 0.2 s; a 0.11 s blend read as a snap (16 degrees a frame).
- **The body drives the stroke.** The solved arm keys change by only a few degrees; the torso twist carries the head across 1.6 studs. Solve each key for the head on its floor target with the torso twist and drop free and the hip gap in the cost. Keep the feet planted and put breakdowns mid-stroke: a 1.2 s scrub of four strokes measured rest 20.6% and foot slide 0.01.
- **Keep the head out of the floor between keys.** Straight from the carry pose to the first floor key the head dug 0.69 under the floor. Breakdowns solved with the grip weight of their time, seeded from the slerp midpoint of the neighbour keys, keep it above. Measure the lowest head corner every frame: it touches the floor within 0.06.
- **Hand back to a running Animator.** When the default Animate still plays under the clip, `Rig:stop` fades to identity and the Animator's pose then pops in. Copy `rig.last`, `stop(0)`, then for 0.15 s write `from:Lerp(motor.Transform, w)` in PreSimulation (the Animator's pose of that frame). Measured in Play: no joint spike at the end.
- **Replication.** Transforms are local, so the server fires a remote with the character and every other client plays the same clip on it.

## A loose object in both hands

A ball or another object that the hands touch but no fist grips.

- **Key the object, not the arms.** Make the object its own joint (a Motor6D from the root that every client adds to the rig's joints, so Poser keys it like a limb) and key it in torso space, so a dip or a lean carries it. Solve the hands onto points on it after the torso in the post pass, with the arm slide clamped to -0.45..0.12. Keyed in root space with the arms chasing it, a ball pulled the arms up to 0.45 out of the shoulders; as a torso-space joint the arms slid at most 0.18.
- **Arc a rising object forward.** A lift or a set travels on an arc in front of the face; a path straight up the chest crossed the body. Put breakdown keys where the hands meet or leave the object.
- **A bounce.** The object speeds up into the floor and eases out going up (a quad in, a quad out): a 0.52 s dribble loop that hits the floor at 0.23 s read as gravity. Put the bounce sound on the contact event, and scale the loop's playback speed with the walk speed (0.75 to 1.5x kept it in step).

## A combo flows from the end of the last swing

- **The fault.** Each swing designed as a good move on its own, from its own ideal load. In a chain, the first frames of the next swing undo the end of the last one (a blade high over one shoulder carried back across the head to a load over the other), and the detour reads as a weird pose even when every key is clean.
- **The rule.** Plan the chain as one path before any key. Key 0 of swing N+1 is swing N's end or hold pose (the same torso, head and blade in torso space), and its first keys carry the blade on in the direction it was travelling or round on its own arc: never back through the guard, never across to a new load. The end of a swing is the load of the next. If a wanted swing needs a load the last swing does not end in, change the last swing's follow-through, not the start of the next one. Start each clip's feet where the last one held them and step them back inside the new clip.
- **An example chain as one path.** Swing 1 chops from the blade laid out behind the right shoulder down across the front and ends low left. Swing 2 starts low left and rises up the diagonal to the blade vertical over the right shoulder. Swing 3 starts there: the body sinks while the blade goes on back over the head, jumps, and drives the blade straight down in front. Swing 4 starts from the slam's end. Check it in Play with a hold on the last swing's end and on the next swing's early keys (0.1, 0.25 and the contact) from the side: the blade must keep travelling one way.
- **A held combo for rhythm.** When players should chain at their own rhythm, end each swing in a moving hold of its end pose (1.5 s worked) before the recovery. Give the hold a middle breath key (the blade lifts a little, the torso rises) and an end key a touch further along the move, with staggered lags (torso -0.08, head +0.1, grip +0.12) so the joints never breathe together; life 0.9. The next swing starts from the hold pose, so a click anywhere in the hold chains cleanly. Make the hold read cocked, not resting: coil the body toward the weapon side (25 degrees of torso turn) with the weapon on the next strike's line. A resting hold reads as the end of the combo; the coil shows the next hit. The combo window equals the hold (the input and the server use the same number), and a finisher's cooldown counts from its chain time, not from the end of its hold. Measure the swing part (up to the chain time) against the one-shot ranges; over the whole clip the hold reads 28 to 55% still but never frozen. Rhythm test: clicks 1.4, 0.7 and 1.9 s apart all chained.
- **Measured after.** Grips 15 degrees off square or less, leg twist 46 or less; in a 60 fps step of every move the only single-frame peaks are the strike frames (blade 60 to 86 degrees a frame with the torso and both arms at their peak on the same frame, then the contact hang). That is a blade turn, not a joint speed; the joint limit for a squeezed strike is in "Measure the blade".

## Slashes that read

- **A slash moves the blade broadside.** A still of a horizontal slash reads as a thrust when the blade points the way it travels, because the blade then slides along its own length. Keep the blade square to the arm and pointing out from the body or trailing the hand; never let the tip lead. With the arm straight out, a square blade that led the hand read as a stab; flipped to trail the hand, the far blade moves almost square to itself and the edge leads. Slash at neck height: an earlier version with the arm down and out (pitch about -60) and the blade level swept at hip and knee height and read wrong.
- **A horizontal slice.** The arm forward near neck height, the blade flat, level and square to the arm, the tip trailing. End with the fist before the opposite shoulder and the blade out past that side, slightly down; an arm that crosses the body passes under the chin. Key the follow-through relative to the torso twist, so a moving variant whose body turns only 55% as far still ends on its own chest. Drive the slice with a big torso turn and a small lunge (torso -46 to +63 degrees, lunge 0.12). Accepted slices measured the tip 3.8 or more above the floor, grips 16 to 21 degrees off square and rest 32 to 36%.
- **Key a slash as an arc.** Give each slash an axis and turn the arm direction about it. The blade is the arm direction crossed with the axis: it trails the hand and stays square to the arm, and the sweep stays broadside. A trailing heavy cut was the fastest limb of its clip (2716 deg/s). The negative of that cross product leads the hand and reads as a stab (above). Where the arm crosses the forward line, drive it with non-Euler channels (Euler branches and the spline).
- **Breakdowns between the swing poses.** A swing keyed pose to pose reads robotic. Between the key poses add an anticipation key, a drag key (the blade trails the hand about 18 degrees), an overshoot past each end pose, a settle and a recovery. Lead with the off arm; trail the weapon arm 1 to 2 frames and the head 2 to 3. With these a slash measured rest 45% and a guard 18 to 21%.
- **A horizontal slash keeps the wrist.** Solve the contact key freely (hand, blade and edge), then hold that grip for every beat of the swing and solve only the arm, with each beat's blade target made by turning the contact blade by the same yaw as the hand. The blade then sweeps with the arm like a lever. Freely solved grips per beat differed by up to 150 degrees and the spline dragged the tip 2.3 studs under the floor. The edge target is the tangent of the sweep (at the contact of a right-to-left cut the edge faces left-back and the flat faces up).
- **The edge leads.** In a cut or a slam the edge faces the direction of travel; a swing led by the flat reads as a slap. A blade held flat to the front looks wide from a front camera; turn the edge from the side to forward during the wind-up (0.28 s), which on a big blade reads as a beat of its own. "Sharp side up" means the blade stands on its edge with the flat to the front (rest edge `(0, 1, 0)` in torso space); check which side is sharp on a render of the mesh first, since a symmetric double-edged blade has no wrong edge. Treat the edge as a soft preference in the solver (weight 10 against 40 for the blade), because a hard edge term fights the hand and blade targets, and log the edge error per key: 1 to 7 degrees on swings, under 10 at rest.
- **Hidden contact frames.** From a camera straight behind, a blade that crosses 4 to 5 studs in front at shoulder height hides inside the torso silhouette at the contact frame; the slash VFX must carry that frame.
- **A shield strapped to the forearm** sits on the outer face: C0 about (-0.72, -0.55, 0) on the left arm with the shield face along arm -X. Aim the forearm across the chest and add a twist term so the outer face points where the shield should face.

## A weapon in locomotion

- **The set.** Give an equippable weapon a draw, a put-away, an armed idle and an armed walk; a set without a put-away is incomplete. Share one lower-body stance between the unarmed and the armed idle, so an equip or a put-away blends only the upper body and never steps.
- **A draw is anticipation and release.** Grip, sink into the pull, rip fast, pop past the rest pose, swing onto the rest; the sink and the pop give the snap. A cinematic draw adds a slowed strain on the grip with a tremble and a slow hero hold (speeds in Cinematic moves): a 1.92 s draw with its hold at a third of normal speed did not freeze, because of the tremble.
- **A rest pose from a reference frame.** Put the frame's arm and blade into the rest pose in torso space (for example the hand out low to the side at arm's length, the blade level past the hand), and let the idle and the walk carry it on the upper body while the legs keep their cycle. The pose then reads in all locomotion, and no blend turns the blade.
- **Welded in idle and walk.** In a locomotion loop the weapon joint holds one constant pose: the mean grip of the idle, shared by the idle and the walk so their blend never turns the blade in the fist. A traced clip that keys the weapon on its own drifts a few degrees against the arm (3.5 degrees measured) and reads as a floating weapon. Keep the weapon a joint, not a weld, when the game may later throw it: a weld cannot fly off the hand. A carry may slide the arm out of its shoulder joint, but the arm box must touch the torso box on every frame: measure the gap per frame and slide the arm onto the torso side (`scripts/blender/contact_fix_bg.py` does this on a channel JSON).
- **A weapon arm in a run pumps.** A frozen weapon arm next to a pumping free arm reads wrong (a 14 degree pump read as frozen). Pump the weapon arm opposite the free arm at about 60% of its range (2 to 60 degrees against the torso measured, -18 to +36 in the world with the lean), one frame behind the free arm, crossing in about 10 degrees and sliding 0.16 up the shoulder on the forward swing for an elbow cue. A long blade needs that smaller swing, which also reads as the weapon's weight. Twist the arm 18 to 26 degrees outward so a forward blade shows beside the body from the player's camera; pointing straight forward it hid behind the body.
- **Tip clearance from the wrist, not a frozen arm.** Let the wrist give up to 15 degrees against the swing (grip lift +15 with the arm back, -14 with the arm forward; grip 14.9 degrees off square) so the blade rocks with the fist; bias the swing forward. Check the tip height over the cycle (at least 1.38 to 1.56 studs above the floor measured) and remember the torso lean adds to the blade's world angle.
- **Which way the blade points in a run is a design choice.** A trailing blade and a blade held forward (the arm forward at the waist cancelling a 22 degree lean, blade pitch -7.4 to +4.3, heading within 6 degrees of straight ahead) both work. Ask before choosing.
- **A strong carrier walks level.** Any constant lean, shoulder drop or head tilt toward a carried weapon reads as the load pulling the body over (a 3.0 degree side lean and a 5.7 degree head tilt read that way). Remove the mean with one constant correction about the hip centre and one on the head, keep the per-step motion, and re-solve the legs to their soles. To stop the blade swaying, hold the weapon arm at its mean world rotation, then give motion back to it as stride pitch: it follows about 60% of the chest twist and pumps 8 degrees opposite the free arm, 2 frames late. Never leave the arm locked in the world: the lock cut the tip sway from 1.46 to 0.04 studs, but the still arm read robotic.
- **A held weapon in a pumping arm is seen best** from a rear three-quarter on the weapon side (58 degrees, 10 studs back, 2.4 up); straight behind, a forward blade and back pieces (a shield, a cape) hide.

## Cinematic moves: speed curves, jumps, spins and root motion

- **Speed curve, not rekeying.** A clip's `warp` (pipeline.md) plays its own clock faster or slower. What read well: the load at 0.75 to 0.85, the strike at 1.3 to 1.5, a hang of 0.3 to 0.45 for 0.03 to 0.05 clip seconds right after the contact, then 1.0 for the recovery. A hero hold in a draw at 0.35 for 0.12 clip seconds (0.34 s real). A strain on a grip at 0.55 with a tremble post layer (noise at 22 per second, 3 degrees on the arm, 1.4 on the torso) so the slow part is not a freeze. Give every slowed beat such a tremble; a slow beat without one reads as paused. Retime a combo the same way: a warp moves the felt timing without moving a key (hits from the first click moved from 0.33 to 2.87 s to 0.25 to 1.58 s, every pose unchanged). Keep hit, lock and chain times on the server from `Poser.realTime`, never from hand-typed seconds.
- **A jump without moving the root.** Lift the torso and lift both feet with an air curve that is at least the rise (0.3 to 0.4 more tucks the legs up into the hips). A lift curve behind the torso opens a hip gap on the takeoff frame (0.12 at 0.15 s); start the lift as the legs straighten. Hand targets in root space must rise with the body, or the arm hangs below the shoulder and the blade tip goes under the floor (0.52 under at an apex). A split in the air (the front foot 0.45 forward, the back foot 0.75 back) reads as a leap; straight legs read as a float.
- **Spins past 360.** Key the torso twist straight through (-68, -12, 75, 165, 255, 325, 388) and keep the Euler branch fix off the torso and head, only on the arms and the grip: a branch fix unwinds a spin. Better, drive the torso and head yaw as an exact function of time instead of Euler keys, so the yaw passes 360 with no branch trouble. Apply the lean after the yaw so the lean turns with the body; a lean applied before the yaw stays fixed in the world. Key the blade in torso space so it rides the spin, with the edge along the spin (the spin axis crossed with the blade). The feet turn round the root with the torso while airborne; land with the chest 25 to 35 degrees past the hips, then pivot one foot at a time back to the stance. A blade held level in torso space points down once the torso leans forward (at a 19 degree lean it went 1.67 under the floor); tilt it up by the lean.
- **A ground spin that travels.** 720 degrees on one foot twists the leg off the torso. Alternate the pivot foot every half turn: each swing foot lifts and lands ahead on the travel line while the planted pivot turns on the spot. The hips lead and the torso trails about 30 degrees, then whips through at the contact; the body stays low and leans away from a heavy blade held out. Measured: 720 degrees in 0.5 s over 8 studs, foot lift 0.3, crouch 0.26 to 0.4, leg twist 47 and 40 degrees, grip 26 or less off square.
- **A charging thrust dash.** Gather; a braced lunge with the back foot slid back (1.45) and planted; the burst with that foot still planted while the torso uncoils, because the planted foot is what the burst pushes off; a flat flight pitched forward (-52 to -58 degrees) with the legs trailing in line and the weapon ahead in both hands; a skid with the tip just above the floor (0.21); a recovery. The lunge crouches 0.64 to 0.7. A 1.40 s clip measured rest 39.2% and contrast 3.9.
- **Spin, dash and travel as clip fields.** For big root motion, give the clip functions of clip time and let the owner client place the root in PreRender: `spin(t)` returns the root yaw (a monotone cubic, so the yaw never runs backward; `K.monotone` in the three-sword kit), `dash = {t0, t1}` moves the root over a raycast-clamped distance with an ease-out, and `root(t)` returns height, forward distance and yaw. A leap is a parabola `h * 4u(1 - u)` so it lands with speed, not an eased stop; a spin that ends on a multiple of 360 faces the target at the release. Feet keys stay in root space, so the air tuck and the landing lunge ride with the root. A 1440 degree spin then needs no per-turn keys; `Poser.check` reads rest high (67%) because the root, not the joints, carries the motion. Key a dash's crossing pose at the middle of the window and the arrival past the target.
- **A dash.** Ease the travel out over the window: distance x (1 - (1 - u)^2). Run the same raycast on the caster and the server and stop short of walls (1.5 studs), so the travel and the hit agree; invulnerability lasts exactly the mover's time. A 12 stud dash over 0.25 s read as a clean dash. For a dash through a target use a short window (30 studs in 0.08 s read as a cut through), cross at the middle, hold past the target with the blades trailing, and land the hit after the pass.
- **Whole-body turn and flip channels.** A `turn = {{t, deg}}` (yaw) and `flip = {{t, deg}}` (pitch) pair that rotates only the root joint (Torso pose = turn * flip * body) lets the arm, weapon and leg poses ride along with no extra keys; author the body as if the channel were zero. A clip that ends in a hover ends the channel on a multiple of 360. A 360 degree flip in 0.3 s read cleanly this way. After a spin, key the torso yaw from the facing you want minus the turn modulo 360, or the follow-through keeps rotating. Rotate a flip around the centre of mass (lesson-videos.md, physics).
- **Busy time.** Let the server's busy window end before the clip ends (the real length minus 0.3 s worked) so the next move can start while the recovery settles.
- **Numbers.** Swings 28 to 47% rest in real time with contrast 3.3 to 5.8; a long cinematic draw or sheathe sits at 62 to 67% rest in real time because of its deliberate slow beats (still 3% or less, frozen 0).

## More than two weapons, and props on the head

- **A free-model blade as a grip joint.** Weld the model to a small invisible handle part named for the joint and scale it about the handle (0.45 made a 5.8 stud katana). For a katana, handle frame +Y from the guard to the pommel and +Z along the edge; the edge sits on the convex side of the curve. Put the fist so the guard stays outside it (origin 0.18 ahead of the guard toward the pommel, `GRIP_Y` 0.35; C0 `(0, -0.9, 0)`, C1 `CF(0, 0.35, 0) * GRIP_R:Inverse()`).
- **A prop on the head** (a blade held in the mouth) is a Motor6D on the Head: C0 `CFrame.fromMatrix((0, -0.24, -0.5), (0, 1, 0), (1, 0, 0))` (pommel to the right, blade out to the left, edge forward), C1 `CF(0, 0.45, 0)` so the guard clears the cheek. It rides the neck; head yaw swings it (+28 turns the blade back for a load, -22 forward for a strike).
- **A stance loop over the Animator's legs.** A stance that keys only the head, the arms and the grips lets the default walk drive the legs. End the move on the loop's first pose with the torso and legs at rest (feet at x +-0.5, z 0) so the hands come back with no snap.
- **Readability from behind.** Blades raised on wide fists with the edge inward (the tips curve out) read as wings from the player's camera; blades pointing back point at the camera and vanish.
- **Low blades after a dash.** A low flick after a dash put a tip through the floor; raise the blade direction (y -0.12 with z -0.99 held it clear) and check the tip on the onion skin.
- **A cast mark from the root joint.** A watcher that fires when the root joint's Transform leaves identity fires again mid-move when a clip passes through identity (in a spin); take the first mark after the key press as the cast start.

## Props that grow, giant props and runtime cost

- **A prop that comes out of another prop.** A hilt rising out of a thin book hangs its guard and tang out of the bottom of the book on every frame before it is fully out. Grow it instead: scale the prop uniformly from 0 to 1 with its lower end pinned to the page, and grow the blade (a MeshPart scaled along its length only) so the tip stays on the page while the hand pulls. The fist then grips the grown hilt, so present the source prop lower (hip height) and further out. A thrown weapon that returns: fly an anchored copy from the release frame (captured from the real handle), let it climb and turn over, drop point first, and shrink the blade with its tip pinned.
- **A giant prop is not the welded weapon.** Resizing a part welded into a character rebuilds the assembly every frame (25 to 30 ms frames for a quarter second). Draw a growing or giant part as an anchored copy placed at the welded part's frame each frame, hide the welded one, and switch back when it stops changing. Low-pass its direction (0.3 s while it grows, 0.05 s in a slam) and clamp how far below level it may point, so a 2 degree wrist error does not swing a 384 stud tip 13 studs.
- **A dramatic overhead slam.** Let the body settle in the idle for about a second, lift, hold the top 2 to 3 s with a growing tremble and a breath, then slam hard. The hold builds anticipation without freezing.
- **Grow, hold, slam as three clips.** A cutscene grow (clip time = real time, events on the reference's shot changes) whose `onDone` plays a looping hold clip (a breath key and a strain tremble on the arms) that stays until the slam replaces it; a loop lasts as long as the player aims. The hold aims by turning the root toward the camera at a limited rate while the Humanoid's AutoRotate is off.

## The twitch when a move hands back to the idle

Two causes, found in order:

1. **Snaps inside the clip.** A sheathe flick turned the torso 36 degrees in one frame (two keys 0.04 apart after a slow warp hold, with opposing torso lags), and a draw's flourish started on the same frame as a leg step. Fix: space the keys, drop the opposing lags, ease the warp out of the hold.
2. **The hand-over itself, which an Edit-mode step never shows.** The Humanoid's Animator zeroes every `Motor6D.Transform` between PreAnimation and PreSimulation (a probe read the right shoulder at 13.6 degrees in PreAnimation and PreRender and 0.0 in PreSimulation). A blend that captured the old pose from `motor.Transform` inside a step (an `onDone` hand-over) blended from the default pose: every joint stopped for one frame and then jumped (the right arm 27.9 degrees, the torso 9.8). `Poser` now keeps the pose each rig last wrote (`rig.last`) and blends from that.

**How to find it.** In Play, record each body part's CFrame relative to the root on every RenderStepped through the end of the move and one second of idle, turn it into degrees per 1/60 s and flag frames over 3 degrees and over 2.2 times both neighbours. Ignore a flag that follows a long frame (a hitch, not the animation).
