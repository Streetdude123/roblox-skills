## Game animation direction research

Checked 44 findings against their sources with an independent re-read; only supported or partly supported findings (with the checker's correction) are used in [game-animation-direction.md](game-animation-direction.md). The sources below were read as text; no video stream was opened.

| Source | Supports |
| --- | --- |
| [Normoyle and Jörg (2014), Trade-offs between responsiveness and naturalness for player characters, Motion in Games (Penn repository record; the PDF was read from the repository's bitstream)](https://repository.upenn.edu/hms/141) | Switch, Blend and MoveTree transition times (0.02 to 0.03 s, 0.31 to 0.37 s, 1.3 and 1.41 s, other MoveTree transitions 0.1 to 0.24 s), the least responsive controller hurting performance and control (time to goal, lives lost), viewers rating MoveTree most natural while players reported no better motion quality, the cape condition enjoyed significantly less for reasons the authors do not know, faster turn playback or transitions from transitions as the authors' fix, and the conclusion that responsiveness should always be prioritized over naturalness. |
| [VGC (April 2024), Stellar Blade director says half-second 'input lag' when parrying and dodging is deliberate](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/stellar-blade-director-says-half-second-input-lag-when-parrying-and-dodging-is-deliberate) | Kim Hyung-tae's 0.5 s delay between the press and the skill on screen, kept for the animation style, the skill activating on the press, and the day-one patch notes (per the game's official X account) on more responsive basic attacks, blocking and parrying. |
| [Jonathan Cooper, Basics: Fast vs Slow (gameanim.com, 2005)](https://www.gameanim.com/2005/06/11/player-control-fast-vs-slow/) | Feel before looks, the three archetypes, anticipation, apex and recoil, longer anticipation for NPCs, timing rules set early in prototyping, Devil May Cry and Resident Evil as successful fast and slow approaches, and a dose of unresponsiveness set per project. |
| [Game Anim Spotlight: Richard Lico (gameanim.com, 2017)](https://www.gameanim.com/2017/01/27/game-anim-interview-richard-lico/) | The follow-through tells what the press did, the anticipation as a pose with a short blend, Destiny's overlapping third-person states, and Moss merging body mechanics with classic Disney exaggeration. |
| [Aztez blog, Characters Adhering To Input (2010)](https://aztez.com/blog/2010/12/19/characters-adhering-to-input/) | Bayonetta's 4 to 6 frames of anticipation on opening attacks before 3 to 30 frames of attack. |
| [Peyman Massoudi, The Challenge of Having Both Responsiveness and Naturalness in Game Animation (Game Developer)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-challenge-of-having-both-responsiveness-and-naturalness-in-game-animation) | Responsiveness costs naturalness, a cancel interval after the move's job is done at 50% of its length, additive land and windmill layers, slower enemies with more frames, run attacks built after a run loop, and the scarf as secondary motion. |
| [Jonathan Cooper, The 12 Principles Of Animation (In Video Games) (gameanim.com, 2019)](https://www.gameanim.com/2019/05/15/the-12-principles-of-animation-in-video-games/) | Many rough animations blending beat one beautiful one, longer anticipation for bigger damage as risk against reward, procedural drag continuing across animations, and the junior mistake of parts starting or arriving together. |
| [Jonathan Cooper, Basics: Making Character-Driving Fun (gameanim.com, 2005)](https://www.gameanim.com/2005/07/03/inertia-making-character-driving-fun/) | Desired against actual speed, turn 180, stop and run-lean animations, deceleration faster than acceleration, turn-rate limits, and inertia hurting twitch games while Mario thrives on it. |
| [Jonathan Cooper, Basics: Mario 64 Case Study (gameanim.com, 2005)](https://www.gameanim.com/2005/08/25/mario-the-epitome-of-character-driving/) | Mario 64's 90 and 180 degree turns and turn-leaning, and his opinion that Mario Sunshine's instant 180 made it suffer. |
| [Roblox-Client-Tracker, BaseCamera.lua (Roblox player scripts)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MaximumADHD/Roblox-Client-Tracker/roblox/scripts/PlayerScripts/StarterPlayerScripts/PlayerModule.module/CameraModule/BaseCamera.lua) | The 12.5 stud default distance (clamped to the zoom limits), the 1.5 stud focus offset above an R6 root part, the 80 degree pitch limits, and CameraRelative rotation in first person and mouse lock. |
| [Roblox-Client-Tracker, MouseLockController.lua (Roblox player scripts)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MaximumADHD/Roblox-Client-Tracker/roblox/scripts/PlayerScripts/StarterPlayerScripts/PlayerModule.module/CameraModule/MouseLockController.lua) | The shift lock camera offset of 1.75 studs to the right. |
| [Roblox-Client-Tracker, ClassicCamera.lua (Roblox player scripts)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MaximumADHD/Roblox-Client-Tracker/roblox/scripts/PlayerScripts/StarterPlayerScripts/PlayerModule.module/CameraModule/ClassicCamera.lua) | The mouse lock offset applied along the camera's right vector in third person. |
| [Roblox creator-docs, RotationType enum](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/enums/RotationType.yaml) | MovementRelative (the default) faces the movement; CameraRelative aligns the body with the camera's look direction. |
| [Roblox creator-docs, Camera class (FieldOfView)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs/main/content/en-us/reference/engine/classes/Camera.yaml) | The vertical field of view defaults to 70 degrees. |
| [Uppsala University game design blog, GDC: Overwatch animation talk summary (David Gibson)](https://babel.speldesign.uu.se/?p=113918) | Eva Sokolova's April 2016 notes on David Gibson's GDC animation bootcamp talk on Overwatch's Mei: total symmetry looks stiff (the idle notes are used through polish-and-critique.md). |
| [80.lv, Animation in Games: Function and Automation (Richard Lico, 2017)](https://80.lv/articles/animation-in-games-function-and-automation) | Readability balancing motion against posing, 5 to 8 frames at 30 fps to see a pose and 2 frames to feel a motion, held key poses with a jitter on supers, a calm idle balancing the run, first animations with a new character rarely feeling right, and poses that defy physics. |
| [Jonathan Cooper, Smash Bros Character Conformity (gameanim.com, 2008, notes on Sakurai's GDC 2008 talk)](https://www.gameanim.com/2008/07/10/smash-bros-character-conformity/) | StandBy, WindUp (never over 0.75 s), Strike and FollowThrough, stage numbers aiding designers, side-on Microman photographs, and consistent parameters, motions and character essence. |
| [Inven Global, How Overwatch's first-person animation breathed life into heroes (Matthew Boehm, GDC 2017)](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/1187/how-overwatchs-first-person-animation-breathed-life-into-heroes) | Matt Boehm's GDC 2017 talk on Overwatch's first-person animation: each hero walking differently (Genji, Junkrat), the third-person view of first-person animations (Reinhardt's hand, Winston's arm), stretched limbs and blades, and reloads kept clear of the player's sight around the reticle. |
| [Riot Games, /dev: Clarity in League (2021)](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/) | Silhouettes for recognition, animation as a bonus differentiator (Lee Sin, Ivern), facing and charge direction at a glance, attention matching importance, hitboxes matching effects, and cosmetics giving no advantage. |
| [Overwatch 2 animation team, Weekly Recall: Animation Station (Blizzard)](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/24239492/weekly-recall-animation-station/) | Movements chosen with the strike team and narrative designers from each hero's history and personality (Soldier: 76 against Mercy), rough drafts sent back to the drawing board, most heroes poised to attack in their idle (Cassidy, Reaper), and Wuyang's agile slam with a spin instead of Reinhardt's overhead pose. |
| [Jonathan Cooper, Layers of Pixar Polish (gameanim.com, 2007, notes on Andy Schmidt's talk)](https://www.gameanim.com/2007/10/13/layers-of-pixar-polish/) | A signature movement style per character and pose character sheets of correct and incorrect methods as a brand bible. |
| [Jonathan Cooper, The Game Animation Test (gameanim.com, 2016)](https://www.gameanim.com/2016/01/25/the-game-animation-test/) | New hires must match the team's style (exaggeration against realism, popping against fluid holds), and much of a style comes from time constraints. |
| [JC Delannoy, Common Mistakes In Game Animation (gameanim.com reprint, 2016; original on Game Developer)](https://www.gameanim.com/2016/09/30/common-mistakes-game-animation/) | No vital pose before the blend builds up, the outgoing clip keeping its momentum through a blend, extra motion scaled on the speed curve, verification scripts over all assets, and joystick input stabilisation. |
| [Jerry Zhang, Anatomy of an Enemy Attack in Dark Souls 3 (Game Developer, 2022)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/anatomy-of-an-enemy-attack-in-dark-souls-3) | The five attack phases, the 340 ms signal and attack minimum, the 170 ms and 240 ms end pose minimums, 2 to 4 active frames and the 60 to 100 ms input response. |
| [Surrender at 20, Red post collection: champion animation (Rory Alderton, Riot, 2014)](https://www.surrenderat20.net/2014/09/red-post-collection-champion-animation.html) | Yasuo's ultimate fixed at a one second, 30 frame cast time, and ultimates larger than life and highly readable. |
| [Perfect Dark Zero: Animation Lead Jon Mummery (gameanim.com reprint of a 2006 interview)](https://www.gameanim.com/2016/08/19/perfect-dark-zero-animation-lead-jon-mummery-interview/) | Daily work with programmers, audio and particles, design asking for length and distance, sound added over the in-game animation, speed tweaks in data, and temporary models animated before final ones. |
| [Hi-Fi Rush Music-Synced Animation (gameanim.com, 2023, CEDEC 2023 talk)](https://www.gameanim.com/2023/09/08/hi-fi-rush-music-synced-animation/) | Animation authored at 120 BPM and 60 fps with key poses on the beat, every element matched to the BPM and sped up or down in sync. |
| [Roblox DevForum, Best way to replicate VFX/Animations for a Battlegrounds game? (2026)](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/4814813) | The client to server to all clients pattern, with every client playing the animation and firing visuals and sounds from its animation events. |
| [Roblox DevForum, What Is The Optimal Way to Handle Animations that use GetMarkerReachedSignal to trigger Remotes? (2020)](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/607956) | Remote-fired effects arriving before the observed animation's marker, and markers still firing on observers' copies of another player's track. |
| [Roblox DevForum, Are animation events safe to use for starting and ending melee hitboxes? (2024)](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/3327434) | A reply recalling that animation events do not fire when the animation fails to load. |
| [Roblox DevForum, How would I go about making my combat feel more impactful? (2022)](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/2042214) | Reviewers noticing particles that appear before the animation plays. |
| [Animation Prototyping For Games (gameanim.com, Jay Hosfelt's GDC 2014 talk)](https://www.gameanim.com/2018/03/21/animation-prototyping-for-games/) | Quick gray-box animation assets for designers' iteration cycles and animators taking part in early prototyping. |
| [Mikhail Vershinin, More Than Movements: Solving Animation Problems (Game Developer, 2024)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/more-than-movements-solving-animation-problems) | Stepped poses and breakdowns before interpolation, approval of all poses before splining, preparation as 60 to 70 percent of the work, overlaps last, beauty not guaranteeing gameplay success, the four-feedback limit, merged blocking and spline, the 20 percent squash limit and eyelids. |
| [Roblox DevForum, Combat Animation Lacks Power (2022)](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/1875328) | A reviewer's note that the character recovers from a punch as if it used as little force as possible. |
| [GameRevolution, GDC 2019: How the God of War team made Kratos more mature (Bruno Velazquez)](https://www.gamerevolution.com/features/513433-god-of-war-mature-kratos-sony-santa-monica) | Bruno Velasquez's GDC 2019 talk (the article's spelling): the 90/10 hand-keyed and mocap split of the old games, mocap for a grounded Kratos, over-the-top proportions suiting the far camera, a Kratos still over the top but more grounded, his grounded walk, and dual swords reined in. |
| [Naughty Dog, Naughty Dog at GDC 2021](https://www.naughtydog.com/blog/naughty_dog_at_gdc_2021) | The Last of Us Part II's switch to the then experimental Motion Matching system and the 3.8 GB slide file that was not read. |
