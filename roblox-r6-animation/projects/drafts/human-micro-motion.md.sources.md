## Life in still poses: sway, breath, gaze and fidgets research

Checked 33 findings (pending independent check).

Open-access full texts were read through the Europe PMC REST service (`https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/<PMCID>/fullTextXML`), except Freedman (2008), read from its PMC page. Abstracts came from NCBI E-utilities (`efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=<PMID>&rettype=abstract`). DevForum threads were read through their JSON view (`https://devforum.roblox.com/t/<id>.json`), the creator docs from the YAML files in the creator-docs repository, the two arXiv papers and the Improv paper as PDF text. No video was opened. Numbers marked "our derivation" in the draft are computed from these sources at 2.86 studs per metre and 60 fps.

| [Błaszczyk and Beck, Posturographic Standards for Optimal Control of Human Standing Posture (Journal of Human Kinetics, 2023)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10170532/) | COP speeds of 100 young adults in a 60 s stance, faster front to back than side to side, and higher with the eyes closed. |
| [Duarte and Freitas, Revision of posturography based on force plate for balance evaluation (Revista Brasileira de Fisioterapia, 2010)](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbfis/a/hFQTppgw4q3jGBCDKV9fdCH/?lang=en) | Shifting, fidgeting and drifting in prolonged unconstrained standing, and a wider base raising stability. |
| [Yoon et al., Factors Affecting Test Results and Standardized Method in Quiet Standing Balance Evaluation (Annals of Rehabilitation Medicine, 2012)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3309333/) | A larger side-to-side COP extent at shoulder-width stance than at half that width, which the authors attribute to lower-leg muscle effort. |
| [Wang, Ko, Challis and Newell, The Degrees of Freedom Problem in Human Standing Posture (PLoS ONE, 2014)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3888423/) | Joint-angle SDs of 0.18 to 0.31 degrees in still standing and 0.57 to 1.78 degrees on one foot or rocking. |

| [Duarte and Sternad, Complexity of human postural control in young and older adults during prolonged standing (Experimental Brain Research, 2008), abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18696056/) | COP fluctuations in 30-minute standing not different from 1/f noise over 10 to 600 s. |
| [Takahashi, Kaneko, Nakazawa et al., Effects of arousal and valence on standing kinematics and kinetics in young men (Physiological Reports, 2025)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12441004/) | Less COM and ankle-angle variability with unpleasant pictures, more tidal volume with high arousal and a faster breath with pleasant pictures. |
| [Bates, McGregor and Alexander, Prolonged standing behaviour in people with joint hypermobility syndrome (BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders, 2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8638551/) | Median fidgets per minute by size of weight moved (10 to 25%, 25 to 50%, over 50%) in 15 minutes of standing by people of normal flexibility. |

| [Prado-Rico and Duarte, Asymmetry of Body Weight Distribution During Quiet and Relaxed Standing Tasks (Motor Control, 2019), abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31088256/) | Relaxed standing alternating between postures, women more asymmetric than men, and two preferred modes of weight distribution. |
| [Freitas, Wieczorek, Marchetti and Duarte, Age-related changes in human postural control of prolonged standing (Gait & Posture, 2005), abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16274914/) | Higher quiet-stance COP RMS and speed after 30 minutes of standing, and smaller postural changes in the elderly. |
| [Davis and Stagg, Interrelationships of the volume and time components of individual breaths in resting man (Journal of Physiology, 1975), abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1142186/) | Resting inspiratory times of 1 to 2.5 s against expiratory times of 2.0 to 3.5 s, and longer inhales for bigger breaths. |
| [Hagio, Obata, Nakazawa et al., Effects of breathing movement on the reduction of postural sway during postural-cognitive dual tasking (PLoS ONE, 2018)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5973601/) | A faster, smaller breath and less sway during mental arithmetic, their correlation, and the cited effects of tidal volume and breath holding on sway. |
| [Spicer and Dennis, The six minute walk test in healthy young women (JSAMS Plus, 2024)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12980604/) | Breath rates at rest and one minute after a six-minute walk, and the 4.0 to 4.5 minute heart-rate recovery. |
| [Zemková, Physiological Mechanisms of Exercise and Its Effects on Postural Sway: Does Sport Make a Difference? (Frontiers in Physiology, 2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8908905/) | The three phases of post-exercise sway recovery, the fast decline with a half-time of about 1 minute tied to ventilation, and a return to baseline over a few minutes. |
| [Andrews, Melnychuk, Dockree et al., Sighs Shape Respiratory Variability and Pupil Dynamics and Adapt to Sustained Attention Demands (Psychophysiology, 2026)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12829434/) | Sighs defined as breaths of at least twice the mean inspiratory volume, 0.45 to 0.63 sighs per minute, mean breath rates, and pupil changes around sighs. |
| [Vlemincx, Severs and Ramirez, The psychophysiology of the sigh: II: The sigh from the psychological perspective (Biological Psychology, 2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13169382/) | More sighs during fear, depression and desire imagery and under mental stress, and sighs as relief. |
| [Stahl, Amplitude of human head movements associated with horizontal saccades (Experimental Brain Research, 1999), abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10333006/) | An eye-only range of 35.8 ± 31.9 degrees, a head slope of 0.77 beyond it, and wide but stable differences between people. |
| [Fang, Nakashima, Matsumiya et al., Eye-Head Coordination for Visual Cognitive Processing (PLoS ONE, 2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4370616/) | Eye-only gaze shifts within ±30 degrees horizontally and ±12 vertically, and several saccades inside one head movement. |
| [Hooge, Niehorster, Nyström et al., Large eye–head gaze shifts measured with a wearable eye tracker and an industrial camera (Behavior Research Methods, 2024)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11335818/) | A 250 ms gaze shift inside a 400 ms head movement, the 40 degree eye range, and which of eye and head starts first for predictable and unpredictable targets. |
| [Freedman, Coordination of the Eyes and Head during Visual Orienting (Experimental Brain Research, 2008)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2605952/) | The head turning on for 250 ms and 15 degrees after the gaze lands, and the head starting first in large or predictable gaze shifts. |
| [Anastasopoulos, Naushahi, Sklavos et al., Fast gaze reorientations by combined movements of the eye, head, trunk and lower extremities (Experimental Brain Research, 2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4369292/) | Durations and peak head speeds of single-step and multiple-step look-backs to 90, 135 and 180 degrees, their shares, and the feet starting 350 ms after the eyes. |
| [Bentivoglio et al., Analysis of blink rate patterns in normal subjects (Movement Disorders, 1997), abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9399231/) | Blink rates of 17 per minute at rest, 26 in conversation and 4.5 while reading. |
