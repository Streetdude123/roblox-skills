## Human motion data: gait, running, strikes and jumps research

Checked 38 findings against their sources with an independent re-read; only supported or partly supported findings (with the checker's correction) are used in [human-motion-data.md](human-motion-data.md). The sources below were read as text; no video stream was opened.

Open-access full texts were read through the Europe PMC REST service (`https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/<PMCID>/fullTextXML`), except Collins et al. (its PMC page), Pontzer et al. and Dorn et al. (the journal pages) and Kellis and Katis (the journal's own site); abstracts came from the Europe PMC search service, and DevForum threads from their JSON view. The links below go to each article's page.

| Source | Supports |
| --- | --- |
| [Brunnekreef et al. 2005, Reliability of videotaped observational gait analysis in patients with orthopedic impairments (BMC Musculoskelet Disord), Table 3](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC555760) | Normal sagittal hip, knee, ankle and pelvis angles by gait phase (hip 25 flexed to 10 extended, knee 20, 40 and 60 degree bends, pelvis 5 degrees forward and back), used for the walk leg and pelvis findings and the ankle line in Does not transfer. |
| [Kinematics of human gait (University of Oklahoma Health Sciences Center course notes)](https://ouhsc.edu/bserdac/dthompso/web/gait/knmatics/gait.htm) | Saunders, Inman and Eberhart's determinants: the pelvis rotated forward on the loading side and back on the push-off side in double support, limiting the drop of the centre of gravity; the notes' caveat that later research questions the determinants. |
| [Fukuchi, Fukuchi and Duarte 2019, Effects of walking speed on gait biomechanics in healthy participants (Syst Rev)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6595586) | In young adults, slower walking cuts knee flexion with a large effect and hip angles with small effects; faster walking raises hip flexion and ankle plantarflexion slightly; cadence and step length change with large effects; 20 studies, 587 people. |
| [Orendurff et al. 2004, The effect of walking speed on center of mass displacement (J Rehabil Res Dev), PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15685471/) | Side-to-side centre of mass motion falling from 6.99 to 3.85 cm and vertical motion rising from 2.74 to 4.83 cm between 0.7 and 1.6 m/s (10 subjects). |
| [Collins, Adamczyk and Kuo 2009, Dynamic arm swinging in human walking (Proc R Soc B)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2817299/) | Minimal shoulder torque for normal arm swing, arm momentum opposite the legs', the 12% and 26% metabolic costs of held and opposite-phase arms, the 63% larger vertical ground reaction moment without arm swing, and the model's slow-walk arm swing at twice the leg frequency. |
| [Pontzer et al. 2009, Control and function of arm swing in human walking and running (J Exp Biol)](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/212/4/523/18953/Control-and-function-of-arm-swing-in-human-walking) | At 1.5 m/s walking and 3.0 m/s running: shoulder rotation 8.1 and 23.75 degrees, head yaw 5.2 and 6.1, pelvis-to-shoulder phase 149 and 94 degrees, a 0.75 s running stride; with the arms folded, 35.68 degrees of shoulder rotation and 11.3 of head yaw in running, no significant walking shoulder change (8.6) and a small walking head yaw rise (6.0); the deltoids firing together. |
| [Jung and Kang 2026, Range of motion changes in upper and lower limb joints according to locomotion speed in healthy adults (Medicine)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13225530) | 155 adults with 16 inertial sensors: shoulder range 27 to 32, 38 to 42 and 43 to 48 degrees in walking, fast walking and running, elbow range in walking and fast walking, and a pelvic rotation range near 20 degrees at every speed. |
| [Roblox DevForum, Alternative R6 Animations (2022)](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/alternative-r6-animations/1684667) | A reply calling an R6 walk that raises the legs above the waist very weird (the poster's opinion), and two replies saying the walk's torso sat too low. |
| [Roblox DevForum, Feedback on my running animation (2019)](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/feedback-on-my-running-animation/315329) | Reviewers calling an R6-style run with a near-still torso and head blocky and stiff, and one suggesting a torso twist. |
| [Jiménez et al. 2026, Reference values for spatiotemporal and kinematic running parameters in recreational runners (J Funct Morphol Kinesiol)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13608732) | Men's medians of cycle, stance time, stance share, stride and vertical oscillation at 7, 9, 11 and 13 km/h, and the foot's contact angle listed in Does not transfer. |
| [Dorn, Schache and Pandy 2012, Muscular strategy shift in human running (J Exp Biol)](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/215/11/1944/10883/Muscular-strategy-shift-in-human-running-dependence) ([PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22573774/)) | Nine runners from 3.5 to 9.0 m/s: stride length leading speed gains up to 7 m/s and stride frequency above it, contact time falling with speed, and flight time peaking at 7 m/s. |
| [Weyand et al. 2000, Faster top running speeds are achieved with greater ground forces not more rapid leg movements (J Appl Physiol), PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11053354/) | An average ground force per body weight 1.26 times higher at 11.1 than at 6.2 m/s top speed, with an unchanged swing time (33 runners). |
| [Nagahara et al. 2014, Kinematics of transition during human accelerated sprinting (Biol Open)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC4133722) | The 0.090 s minimum support time, 4.87 Hz step frequency, 10.04 m/s top speed, the breakpoints at steps 4.4 and 14.1, feet behind the centre of gravity and no stance knee flexion before the first, the centre of gravity and head angle rises, and the upper trunk at 75.7 degrees from horizontal. |
| [Kovše et al., Alterations in pelvic kinematics with speed, incline, and fatigue in female runners (Front Sports Act Living)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12852323) | Running pelvic rotation range of 14 to 19 degrees, obliquity of 14 to 17, tilt range of 8 to 9, and toe-off near 40% of the cycle. |
| [Storniolo et al. 2025, Impact of muscle fatigue on anticipatory postural adjustments during gait initiation (Front Physiol)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC11770096) | The 46.6 mm backward shift of the centre of pressure with its first peak 449 ms after its onset, the forward diving strategy, and the need to push the centre of gravity forward. |
| [Hitchens et al. 2026, Early horizontal deceleration ability across multiple steps and approach speeds (Eur J Sport Sci)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13408429) | Decelerations of 4.3 to 5.1 m/s^2 from 5.1 to 7.1 m/s, peak vertical forces of 4.1 to 5.3 body weights, the first three steps as about half of a stop, and the step 1 and steps 2 to 3 postures that went with more braking force. |
| [Khobkhun and Thanakamchokchai 2023, Biological sex-related differences in whole-body coordination during standing turns (Sci Rep)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10719272) | Men's and women's median head, thorax, pelvis, leading foot and trailing foot onsets of fast 180 degree turns (1.5, 2 and 3 s), the 24 to 27 degree head lead, and 2.5 steps per fast turn. |
| [Tillman, Molino and Zaferiou 2023, Gait-phase specific transverse-plane momenta generation during 90 degree turns while walking (Sci Rep)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10133231) | Late-cued turns being 73% step turns, the push into a step turn during the outside foot's single support, and the lean outside the inside foot in spin turns. |
| [Weiss, Moudy and Wood 2025, Development of head-trunk coordination measures using wearable sensors (Sci Rep)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12820101) | The head leading the trunk by 10 to 20 degrees in planned 90 degree walking turns (citing Sreenivasa et al.) and by a median 38.4 degrees in a cue-driven turn made mid-walk. |
| [Wan and Liu 2026, Kinematic and kinetic differences between lead and rear straight punches in elite boxers (BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13285485) | Straight-punch durations of 0.442 and 0.667 s from the lowest knee bend, trunk ranges and peak forward speeds, peak and contact fist speeds, and each punch loading its own side's leg. |
| [Xu, Mao and Xi 2024, A comparative analysis of punching in boxing and sanda (Front Sports Act Living)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC11466798) | Cross and uppercut hand speeds, hip-line, trunk and shoulder-line angular speeds, the forward and back range of the centre of mass in the cross, and right-side knee and shoulder angles at peak punch speed. |
| [Dinu and Louis 2020, Biomechanical analysis of the cross, hook, and uppercut in junior vs. elite boxers (Front Sports Act Living)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC7739747) | The elbow contributing most to the cross and the shoulder to the hook and uppercut, junior and elite differences, the hook's higher speed, and the lead-to-rear force split estimated from the centre of mass at impact. |
| [Escamilla et al. 2025, Pitching biomechanics on dirt and turf mounds (Orthop J Sports Med)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12657847) | Peak pelvis and upper-trunk speeds and their timing as a share of foot contact to release, elbow and shoulder speeds, stride length and knee height. |
| [Scarborough et al. 2021, Comparison of kinematic sequences during curveball and fastball pitches (Front Sports Act Living)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC8459924) | None of 71 curveballs following the full five-segment order (and the cited earlier fastball result), pelvis then trunk then arm holding in the closest and most common orders, and 49% and 43% altered distal orders. |
| [Labbe et al. 2025, Kinematic sequencing of the football pass using inertial motion analysis (Int J Sports Phys Ther)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12317801) | The trunk turned 40 degrees back at foot contact and 21 the other way, about 60 degrees in all, about 20 degrees of hip-to-shoulder separation (11 at release), the lean away from the throwing arm, the stride knee bend and the lumbar extension. |
| [Vagner et al. 2023, A systematic review of dynamic forces and kinematic indicators of front and roundhouse kicks (Sports)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10459763) | Foot speeds of front and roundhouse kicks by level, the elite roundhouse hip and knee speeds, knee extension speed, and total roundhouse execution times. |
| [Kellis and Katis 2007, Biomechanical characteristics and determinants of instep soccer kick (J Sports Sci Med)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3786235/) (text read at [jssm.org](https://www.jssm.org/hf.php?id=jssm-06-154.xml)) | The thigh peaking at 516 to 573 deg/s before the knee extends and slowing to near zero at contact while the shank and foot peak, and backswing hip extension up to 29 degrees. |
| [Razm et al. 2026, Mechanical efficiency and injury risk in leg kicks across combat sports (Healthcare)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12940734) | Pivoted support legs allowing more hip rotation and power while loading the support knee (23 studies). |
| [Chen et al. 2025, Effect of sequential kicks on programming time and movement time in taekwondo (J Hum Kinet)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12360942) | Round kick movement times of 560 and 610 ms from the first (thenar) muscle activity, the double kick's 251 to 269 ms between its contacts, the 885 ms from the double kick's last contact to the turning kick's, and the 149 to 191 ms premotor times. |
| [Ho et al. 2026, Stiffness-altered compression garments and countermovement jump kinematics (J Exerc Sci Fit)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13571423) | Countermovement depth of 0.36 m, jump height, time to takeoff, push time, takeoff and peak downward speeds, hip range and hip extension speed (62 adults, arms crossed). |
| [Spires et al. 2026, Contributions of upper- and lower-body power to countermovement jump performance with and without arm swing (Sports)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13416712) | Jumps of 39.7 with arm swing against 30.7 without, and the cited 10 to 30% gain from earlier work. |
