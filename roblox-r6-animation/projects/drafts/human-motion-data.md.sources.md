## Human motion data: gait, running, strikes and jumps research

Checked 35 findings (pending independent check).

Open-access full texts were read through the Europe PMC REST service (`https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/<PMCID>/fullTextXML`); the links below go to each article's page.

| Source | Supports |
| --- | --- |
| [Brunnekreef et al. 2005, Reliability of videotaped observational gait analysis in patients with orthopedic impairments (BMC Musculoskelet Disord), Table 3](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC555760) | Normal sagittal hip, knee, ankle and pelvis angles by gait phase (hip 25 flexed to 10 extended, knee 20 and 60 degree bends, pelvis 5 degrees forward and back), used for the walk leg and pelvis findings and the ankle line in Does not transfer. |
| [Kinematics of human gait (University of Oklahoma Health Sciences Center course notes)](https://ouhsc.edu/bserdac/dthompso/web/gait/knmatics/gait.htm) | Saunders, Inman and Eberhart's determinants: the pelvis rotated forward on the loading side and back on the push-off side in double support, limiting the drop of the centre of mass. |
| [Fukuchi, Fukuchi and Duarte 2019, Effects of walking speed on gait biomechanics in healthy participants (Syst Rev)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC6595586) | Slower walking cuts knee flexion with a large effect and hip angles with small effects; faster walking raises hip flexion and ankle plantarflexion slightly; 20 studies, 587 people. |
| [Orendurff et al. 2004, The effect of walking speed on center of mass displacement (J Rehabil Res Dev), PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15685471/) | Side-to-side centre of mass motion falling from 6.99 to 3.85 cm and vertical motion rising from 2.74 to 4.83 cm between 0.7 and 1.6 m/s. |
| [Collins, Adamczyk and Kuo 2009, Dynamic arm swinging in human walking (Proc R Soc B)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2817299/) | Nearly passive arm swing opposite the legs, the 12% and 26% metabolic costs of held and wrong-phase arms, and the model's slow-walk arm swing at twice the leg frequency. |
| [Pontzer et al. 2009, Control and function of arm swing in human walking and running (J Exp Biol)](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/212/4/523/18953/Control-and-function-of-arm-swing-in-human-walking) | Shoulder rotation 8.1 degrees walking and 23.75 running, head yaw 5.2 and 6.1, pelvis-to-shoulder phase 149 and 94 degrees, more chest and head turn with the arms restrained in running, and the deltoids firing together. |
| [Jung and Kang 2026, Range of motion changes in upper and lower limb joints according to locomotion speed in healthy adults (Medicine)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13225530) | Shoulder range 27 to 32, 38 to 42 and 43 to 48 degrees in walking, fast walking and running, elbow range in walking, and a pelvic rotation range near 20 degrees at every speed. |
| [Roblox DevForum, Alternative R6 Animations (2022)](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/alternative-r6-animations/1684667) | Replies saying an R6 walk that raises the legs above the waist looks very weird and that a walk's torso sat too low. |
| [Roblox DevForum, Feedback on my running animation (2019)](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/feedback-on-my-running-animation/315329) | Reviewers calling an R6-style run with a near-still torso and head blocky and stiff, and suggesting a torso twist. |
| [Jiménez et al. 2026, Reference values for spatiotemporal and kinematic running parameters in recreational runners (J Funct Morphol Kinesiol)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13608732) | Cycle, stance time, stance share, stride and vertical oscillation at 7, 9, 11 and 13 km/h, and the foot's contact angle listed in Does not transfer. |
| [Dorn, Schache and Pandy 2012, Muscular strategy shift in human running (J Exp Biol)](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/215/11/1944/10883/Muscular-strategy-shift-in-human-running-dependence) ([PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22573774/)) | Stride length leading speed gains up to 7 m/s and stride frequency above it, contact time falling with speed, and flight time peaking at 7 m/s. |
| [Weyand et al. 2000, Faster top running speeds are achieved with greater ground forces not more rapid leg movements (J Appl Physiol), PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11053354/) | Faster runners pressing 1.26 times harder with an unchanged swing time across top speeds of 6.2 to 11.1 m/s. |
| [Nagahara et al. 2014, Kinematics of transition during human accelerated sprinting (Biol Open)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC4133722) | The 0.090 s minimum support time, 4.87 Hz step frequency, 10.04 m/s top speed, the shifts at steps 4 and 14, feet behind the centre of gravity and no stance knee flexion before step 4, and the upper trunk at 75.7 degrees. |
| [Kovše et al., Alterations in pelvic kinematics with speed, incline, and fatigue in female runners (Front Sports Act Living)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12852323) | Running pelvic rotation range of 14 to 19 degrees, obliquity of 14 to 17, tilt range of 8 to 9 around 20 to 24 degrees of forward tilt, and toe-off near 40% of the cycle. |
| [Storniolo et al. 2025, Impact of muscle fatigue on anticipatory postural adjustments during gait initiation (Front Physiol)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC11770096) | The 46.6 mm backward shift of the centre of pressure peaking 449 ms after its onset before heel-off, and the forward diving strategy. |
| [Hitchens et al. 2026, Early horizontal deceleration ability across multiple steps and approach speeds (Eur J Sport Sci)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13408429) | Decelerations of 4.3 to 5.1 m/s^2 from 5.1 to 7.1 m/s, peak vertical forces of 4.1 to 5.3 body weights, more hip and knee flexion in the first braking step, and lower, further-ahead plants in later steps. |
| [Khobkhun and Thanakamchokchai 2023, Biological sex-related differences in whole-body coordination during standing turns (Sci Rep)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10719272) | The head, thorax, pelvis, leading foot and trailing foot onsets of 180 degree turns in 1.5, 2 and 3 s, the 24 to 27 degree head lead, and 2.5 steps per fast turn. |
| [Tillman, Molino and Zaferiou 2023, Gait-phase specific transverse-plane momenta generation during 90 degree turns while walking (Sci Rep)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10133231) | Late-cued turns being 73% step turns, the push into a step turn during the outside foot's single support, and the lean outside the inside foot in spin turns. |
| [Weiss, Moudy and Wood 2025, Development of head-trunk coordination measures using wearable sensors (Sci Rep)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12820101) | The head leading the trunk by 10 to 20 degrees in planned 90 degree walking turns (citing Sreenivasa et al.) and by a median 38.4 degrees in a cue-driven turn. |
| [Wan and Liu 2026, Kinematic and kinetic differences between lead and rear straight punches in elite boxers (BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13285485) | Straight-punch durations of 0.442 and 0.667 s, trunk ranges and speeds, peak and contact fist speeds with a 20 to 23% loss, and each punch loading its own side's leg. |
| [Xu, Mao and Xi 2024, A comparative analysis of punching in boxing and sanda (Front Sports Act Living)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC11466798) | Cross and uppercut hand speeds, hip-line, trunk and shoulder-line angular speeds, centre of mass travel during the cross, and knee and shoulder angles at peak punch speed. |
| [Dinu and Louis 2020, Biomechanical analysis of the cross, hook, and uppercut in junior vs. elite boxers (Front Sports Act Living)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC7739747) | The elbow contributing most to the cross and the shoulder to the hook and uppercut, junior and elite differences, and the lead-to-rear force split of each punch. |
| [Escamilla et al. 2025, Pitching biomechanics on dirt and turf mounds (Orthop J Sports Med)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12657847) | Peak pelvis and upper-trunk speeds and their timing as a share of foot contact to release, elbow and shoulder speeds, stride length and knee height. |
| [Scarborough et al. 2021, Comparison of kinematic sequences during curveball and fastball pitches (Front Sports Act Living)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC8459924) | No pitch following the full five-segment order, pelvis then trunk then arm holding, and 43 to 49% altered distal orders. |
| [Labbe et al. 2025, Kinematic sequencing of the football pass using inertial motion analysis (Int J Sports Phys Ther)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12317801) | The trunk turned 40 degrees back at foot contact and 21 the other way, about 60 degrees in all, 20 degrees of hip-to-shoulder separation, the lean away from the throwing arm, the lead knee bend and the lumbar extension. |
| [Vagner et al. 2023, A systematic review of dynamic forces and kinematic indicators of front and roundhouse kicks (Sports)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC10459763) | Foot, knee and hip speeds of front and roundhouse kicks by level, knee extension speed, and total roundhouse execution times. |
| [Kellis and Katis 2007, Biomechanical characteristics and determinants of instep soccer kick (J Sports Sci Med)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3786235/) | The thigh peaking at 516 to 573 deg/s before the knee extends and slowing to near zero at contact while the shank and foot peak, and backswing hip extension up to 29 degrees. |
| [Razm et al. 2026, Mechanical efficiency and injury risk in leg kicks across combat sports (Healthcare)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12940734) | Pivoted support legs allowing more hip rotation and power while loading the support knee. |
| [Chen et al. 2025, Effect of sequential kicks on programming time and movement time in taekwondo (J Hum Kinet)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12360942) | Round kick movement times of 560 and 610 ms, the double kick's 251 to 269 ms between contacts, the 885 ms turning kick and the 149 to 191 ms premotor times. |
| [Ho et al. 2026, Stiffness-altered compression garments and countermovement jump kinematics (J Exerc Sci Fit)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13571423) | Countermovement depth of 0.36 m, jump height, time to takeoff, push time, takeoff and peak downward speeds, hip range and hip extension speed. |
| [Spires et al. 2026, Contributions of upper- and lower-body power to countermovement jump performance with and without arm swing (Sports)](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13416712) | Jumps of 39.7 with arm swing against 30.7 without, and the cited 10 to 20% gain from earlier work. |
