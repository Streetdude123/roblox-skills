## Anime craft: timing, acting and action on six parts research

Checked 49 findings against their sources with an independent re-read; only supported or partly supported findings (with the checker's correction) are used in [anime-craft.md](anime-craft.md). The sources below were read as text; no video stream was opened.

| Source | Supports |

| [Current Trends in Action Animation: Hiroto Nagata and Ryuki Hashimoto (Animétudes)](https://animetudes.com/2021/08/07/current-trends-in-action-animation-hiroto-nagata-and-ryuki-hashimoto/) | Nagata's even timing on ones and twos, Hashimoto's rhythm built from spacing against a 3DCG background on ones, his dropkick that goes from ones to threes to an irregular fall, and the distorted outlines and shading folds that cannot move to R6. |
| [The Kanada Style Now (Animétudes)](https://animetudes.com/2021/07/09/the-kanada-style-now/) | Yutaka Nakamura's focus on rhythm over timing, a Shingo Yamashita sequence on ones with threes on some impacts, and Takeshi Maenami's swing with no anticipation in a 2020 Monster Strike music video. |
| [The Rise of Realism (Animétudes)](https://animetudes.com/2021/05/28/the-rise-of-realism/) | Umetsu's uniform timing and even spacing reading as tedious, Mitsuo Iso's little gestures and secondary movements in the opening of War in the Pocket, and Satoru Utsunomiya's small, irregular movements on Windaria. |
