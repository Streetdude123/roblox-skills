## Polish and critique: what separates professional motion research

| Source | Supports |
| --- | --- |
| [Victor Navone, Splinophilia Part 1: Timing](http://www.navone.org/HTML/Tutorial_Splines1_2.htm) | An ease key sits at 85 to 95% of the change between two poses, and a symmetrical ease-in and ease-out is less interesting than favouring one pose. |
| [Victor Navone, Splinophilia Part 1: Timing (continued)](http://www.navone.org/HTML/Tutorial_Splines1_3.htm) | Fast-outs and fast-ins (linear or broken tangents) suit motion driven by an external force: a struck ball fast-outs, a ball rolling into a wall fast-ins. |
| [Victor Navone, Splinophilia Part 2: Spline hygiene](http://www.navone.org/HTML/Tutorial_Splines2.htm) | Keys hold the extremes (curve containment), handles that overshoot other keys in time cause hitches, redundant keys go after blocking, contacts keep dense keys, and repetitive curves look repetitive. |
| [Victor Navone, Splinophilia Part 2: Tips and tricks](http://www.navone.org/HTML/Tutorial_Splines2_2.htm) | Copying a curve down a chain with 1 frame per joint (more as needed) and copying a head's tilt onto its turn 5 frames apart gives quick overlap that still needs custom keys at the ends. |
| [Keith Lango, Offset keys vs. changing shapes](http://keithlango.blogspot.com/2009/02/offset-keys-vs-changing-shapes.html) | End-key offsets give overlap at the end but no drag in the middle, drag comes from breakdowns whose shapes change, and per-channel offsets belong to the final polish. |
| [Shawn Kelly, Animation Tips and Tricks Volume 1 (Animation Mentor PDF)](https://content.animationmentor.com/pdfs/TipsAndTricks_Volume1.pdf) | Breakdowns set the timing against the default bell curve, 20 or more frames between breakdowns risk floatiness, a key every 4 or 5 frames, the reach that overshoots up and arcs down, over-arcs and under-arcs, and reversals. |
| [Animation Mentor newsletter, June 2009: What is a moving hold?](https://newsletters.animationmentor.com/newsletter/0609/feature_geek.html) | The overshoot hold and the long ease-in hold, the overshoot pose a couple of frames after the real pose, the invisible-wall stop, ambient motion, and eyes as the life of a hold. |
| [Scott McWhinnie, Breaking down breakdowns (AnimSchool)](https://blog.animschool.edu/2024/08/23/breaking-down-breakdowns/) | The sharper and more abrupt an action, the greater the overshoot; spacers set the eases and the slow-down at a change of direction. |
| [Louaye Moulayess, Adding vibration to animation (AnimSchool)](https://blog.animschool.edu/2024/08/14/adding-vibration-to-animation/) | A struggle vibration peaks at the hardest moment, starts and ends at zero, alternates every frame, and is copied to other controls at a smaller amplitude. |
| [Ricky Renna, Cleaning up curves: splining 101 (AnimSchool)](https://blog.animschool.edu/2024/08/02/cleaning-up-curves-splining-101/) | Stepped keys every three or four frames before splining, auto tangents, the biggest controllers first, and splining in sections of 30 to 40 frames. |
| [Martin Scotto, Avoiding spline depression (AnimSchool)](https://blog.animschool.edu/2025/06/20/avoiding-spline-depression/) | Delete keys down to the essential poses, and default auto tangents do not account for timing, weight or intent. |
| [Jean-Luc Delhougne, Blocking plus workflow: breakdowns (AnimSchool)](https://blog.animschool.edu/2025/08/22/blocking-plus-workflow-breakdowns/) | Before a big motion, shape the moving hold with the hips down and forward and slight opposition in the spine and head. |
| [Gwilym Morris, Acting through anticipation (AnimSchool)](https://blog.animschool.edu/2024/07/19/acting-through-anticipation/) | A surprise gag leaves the anticipation out, and anticipation can mislead the audience about weight. |
| [Jonathan Cooper, The 12 principles of animation in video games (Game Developer)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/the-12-principles-of-animation-in-video-games) | Clean arcs everywhere look light, the head snaps after the body stops in a punch, exaggerated pistol kickback keeps the response instant, held apexes and hyper-real poses, and no cheating to one camera in games. |
| [Jonathan Cooper, essay on blending (gameanim.com)](https://www.gameanim.com/?p=102) | Blended cycles must sync and start on the same pose, and a walk blended with a jog loses its bounce because their high and low frames are opposite. |
| [Overwatch 2 team, Weekly Recall: Animation Station (Blizzard)](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/24239492/weekly-recall-animation-station/) | Common movements are polished first, a light staff ricochets where a heavy hammer lingers, swings arc over the shoulder, and a staff aimed along the body's facing breaks the head-on silhouette. |
| [GDC 2016 Overwatch animation talk, attendee notes (babel.speldesign.uu.se)](https://babel.speldesign.uu.se/?p=113918) | David Gibson's snappy timing (overshoot the extreme, hold key poses a little longer, different timing on the two arms), idles that state the personality, and rigs broken for smears. |
| [11 Second Club, Helpful Hints: The top 5](https://www.11secondclub.com/helpful_hints/the_top_5/) | The five most posted contest comments, the swimmy fix by removing in-betweens (158 frames to 77), heads and hands as the usual floaty parts, the poster-frame pose, and exaggeration as intensity rather than size. |
| [11 Second Club, Helpful Hints: Back and forth](https://www.11secondclub.com/helpful_hints/back_and_forth/) | Flipping between consecutive keys shows whether every body part changes with the main action. |
| [11 Second Club, Helpful Hints: Every frame is a drawing](https://www.11secondclub.com/helpful_hints/every_frame_is_a_drawing/) | Judging randomly chosen frames, mostly in-betweens, as finished drawings. |
