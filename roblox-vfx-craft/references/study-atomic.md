# Study: "Shadow Fake atomic" (a Roblox "I AM ATOMIC" recreation he sent, 2026-09-24)

He sent this 15.6 s clip (1920 x 1080, 60 fps) with "What we should take note on how this guy uses vfx to make his VFX look amazing, improve the water vfx significantly please it needs to look just amazing and anime like". Read frame by frame (contact sheets every 0.5 s, then every 0.2 to 0.3 s on the build-up, the middle and the climax). What makes it look amazing, in order of weight:

1. **A dark stage graded to the effect's hue.** Every shot sits in a near-black, blue-violet world (sky, floor grid and walls all tinted). The effects are the only bright things, so they read as light. On a pale day map the same particles read as grey smoke. Contrast is the first tool, not particle count.
2. **One hue family and a white-hot core.** Blue to violet to magenta, with white where the energy is densest (the pillar core, the blast centre, the dome rim). Colour sits on the edges of the bright shapes (white -> magenta -> blue). A second hue (red, green) appears only inside impact frames, as an inverted flash.
3. **Thin lines everywhere.** Falling thin streaks in the background, zig-zag lightning arcs, concentric thin rings round the body, circuit lines on the floor, radial speed lines at the frame edges. Thin, long, bright lines give direction and energy; they contrast with the few big soft shapes.
4. **An ambient layer in every frame.** Small star specks and slow falling streaks fill the air in every shot, even the close-ups. There is no empty frame.
5. **Depth.** Foreground energy and smoke sweep past the lens (close-ups of the arm with magenta smoke crossing in front), a mid layer on the body, and a background layer. Particles near the camera sell scale.
6. **Rim light on the body.** The character carries a cyan-blue edge light on the shoulders and arms in the effect's colour, so the silhouette separates from the dark stage.
7. **Anime / cel textures.** Flames and smoke are hard-edged and posterized (white, magenta, blue bands), not soft photographic smoke. Impact frames are flat 2 to 3 colour ink shapes (red/white/magenta starburst, a green/yellow/black inverted frame, a blue/magenta threshold frame of the character).
8. **Screen treatment.** Heavy bloom (whites blow out), a vignette, chromatic aberration (red/blue fringes) on close-ups, radial speed lines during the blast.
9. **Shape language.** Verticals (the pillar, falling lines), circles (a dome shockwave outline, concentric rings, a glowing horizon arc), radial rays out of the centre. Few shapes, repeated.
10. **Ground interaction.** The floor under the pillar glows white-lavender, dark cracks radiate from the centre, rocks float up, sparks run along the floor.
11. **Editing and timing.** Black -> slow build with the ambient layer -> an impact frame on each voice beat -> a full white frame -> the climax (pillar, dome, blast) -> a hard cut to a quiet dark shot. Extreme close-ups, low angles looking up, Dutch angles, cuts every 0.5 to 1 s.

Text cards ("I", "AM", "ATOMIC") are part of that clip but he rejected text on his own ultimate ("text ruins it"); do not copy them.

## How it maps to a MOVE rung (the Water Mage M1s)

Transferable without taking the camera: the white-hot core with coloured edges (2), thin lines (3: water streaks along the laser, thin spiral streams, spray lines), a local ambient layer round the caster while casting (4), foreground droplets (5), a rim light on the caster and the target in the effect colour (6), cel-shaded water with hard bands and white foam (7), rings and a shockwave outline at the muzzle and the impact (9), water on the ground at the impact (10). The stage darkening (1), impact frames and flashes (7, 8) and cuts (11) belong to the cinematic rung; on a move they need his yes first.
